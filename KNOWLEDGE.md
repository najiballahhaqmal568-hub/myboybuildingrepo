# ⚠️ EVIDENCE LAYER NOTICE — READ FIRST (2026-08-24 audit)

These notes are distilled from practitioner videos and single studies. Treat every number as a **starting hypothesis**, not a universal law. Evidence quality is mixed: practitioner opinion, EMG, single trials, and meta-analyses appear side by side.

Key corrections from the 2026-08-24 external audit (with primary sources):
- Volume ranges (10–20, MRV 18–30, 52-set outliers) are individual starting points, not laws. ACSM position stand: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/ · weekly-volume review: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/
- Proximity to failure: failure not required for growth; RIR estimation is error-prone (esp. novices/light loads). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33337690/
- Protein: ~1.6 g/kg/day breakpoint meta-analysis; 1 g/lb is an upper end, not a requirement. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/
- Cold-water immersion immediately post-lifting may blunt hypertrophy — effect is context-dependent, not an absolute ban. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35068365/
- Deloads are a low-research, consensus-defined stress-management tool, not a fixed calendar. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37730925/
- "Overtraining is almost nonexistent" and 52-set claims: single-population findings — do not generalize; individual fatigue governs.
- Sleep/protein doubling after one bad night, mouth taping (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40397877/ — limited evidence, asphyxiation risk with nasal blockage), RHR>75 pseudo-diagnosis, FFMI urgency, ketones after head trauma (use CDC red-flag path: https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/signs-symptoms/index.html), and precise supplement-effect promises: treat as hypotheses or remove from product decisions.
- BFR and supplement dosing require individual screening and qualified supervision (AIS BFR guide; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31710505/ · NIH ODS magnesium/omega-3 fact sheets). Hydration needs are individual (NATA: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28985128/).
- ACWR is a load-change indicator, not a validated injury predictor (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32572824/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32502973/).

Conflicting-school rules (RIR strategy, deload placement, volume counting) are decided in POLICY.md — the engine implements one protocol; alternatives here are labeled options.

# FHP Hypertrophy Fundamentals — Per-Muscle Series (Batch 2 of Mega-Library)

Source: Najib's playlist #16 — the 10 "Fundamentals" editions (newer/deeper than the Guidelines batch 1; these include anatomy, indirect-volume counting rules, and study evidence). All processed. Raw transcripts in [transcripts-fundamentals/](transcripts-fundamentals/). Complements [../NOTES.md](../NOTES.md) (guidelines batch) and [../rp-specialization/NOTES.md](../rp-specialization/NOTES.md).

## Abdominals
- Four muscles: rectus (trunk flexion), obliques (lateral flexion/rotation), transverse (stabilization).
- Categories: flexion (crunches, hanging leg raises, machine crunches) · lateral flexion (side bends) · rotation (Russian twists, landmine) · anti-movement (planks, ab wheel, Pallof).
- Technique: ensure SPINAL flexion not just hip flexion (posteriorly tilt pelvis in hanging raises); planks with posterior pelvic tilt beat anterior-tilt versions for ab activity.
- Notable: obliques widen the waist, rectus thickens the front — cap oblique volume if a small waist is the goal. Definition = diet.

## Calves
- Gastrocnemius (2 heads, visual ball; knee-flexor too) vs soleus (deep, lower shape).
- Standing/straight-knee raises best for gastroc; seated/bent-knee = soleus-biased. Standing beat seated for BOTH gastroc heads over 12 weeks.
- Technique: heel drop off a step for full stretch · slow eccentric + ~1s bottom pause (blocks Achilles rebound) · bottom-range partials after failure slightly out-grew full sets over 10 weeks · feet out→medial, in→lateral.
- Volume scales with genetics: good calves may need little; poor responders need heavy workload.

## Triceps
- Long head (scapula origin — also shoulder extender), lateral + medial (humerus origin). Presses fully train lateral/medial but undertrain long head (bi-articular escape).
- **Volume counting rule: each pressing set ≈ 0.5 triceps set**; triceps-biased presses (close-grip bench, upright dips) count full.
- Must include an overhead/skull-crusher movement: overhead extensions BEAT pushdowns for total growth over 12 weeks — extra gains localized to the long head.
- Cable setup matters: resistance should pull parallel to the upper arm into full flexion (lean forward on pushdowns).

## Biceps ✅ (replaces corrupt transcript from batch 1)
- Biceps brachii (elbow flexion + shoulder flexion + supination) + brachialis beneath (pure flexion; its growth pushes the biceps up and adds girth).
- **Volume counting rule: each back set ≈ 0.5 biceps set** (10 back sets ≈ 5 indirect).
- Grip choice barely matters (avoid pure overhand). Cheat curls (~2× load) grew biceps equally to strict — go stricter only if elbows ache.
- Lengthened-vs-shortened evidence is mixed; possible regional effect (bottom-heavy → distal, top-heavy → proximal) — include variety. Drop sets matched traditional sets in far less time.

## Glutes
- Glute max (hip extension) + medius/minimus (abduction).
- Tools: squat patterns · deadlift variants · hip thrusts · back extensions (pad below hips) · abduction machine.
- **Squats and hip thrusts grew glute max EQUALLY over 9 weeks — but neither touched medius/minimus**: abduction isolation is non-negotiable if side-hip matters.
- Deep squats out-grew partials for glutes/adductors. Forward lean / higher leg-press feet bias glutes.
- When loads get silly (heavy extensions, cable-abduction instability): <1-min rests, drop sets, myo-reps.

## Shoulders/Delts
- Front (flexion) / middle (abduction) / rear (extension) + traps.
- Direct volume ≈ HALF what you give chest/back; adjust by response — delts soak huge indirect work.
- Arm rotation switches emphasis: palms-forward → front delts; palms-back → rear/traps. Extend lateral-raise ROM across the body's midline (cable at knee height or lean away). Post-failure lengthened partials on free-weight raises.
- Cables ≈ dumbbells for lateral raise growth (8 wks). Shrugs redundant with rows + reverse flies. If you can do only ONE delt exercise: lateral raise covers everything best.

## Hamstrings
- Three muscles, all knee-flexors; all hip-extensors EXCEPT biceps-femoris short head (trained ONLY by leg curls).
- Two families: leg curls (seated/lying/Nordic — complete coverage) + hip hinges (SLDL/good mornings/hypers — miss short head).
- **Seated leg curl BEAT lying leg curl** (hip flexion = training at long lengths); only short head grew equally both ways.
- Hinges: minimal knee bend for max stretch; stop at TECHNICAL failure (form breaks under load); curls may go past true failure. Ankle position irrelevant; foot rotation mildly biases inner/outer.

## Back
- Upper back (traps/rear delts/rhomboids — scapular retraction) · lats/teres (shoulder extension/adduction) · erectors (spinal extension).
- Rows bias upper back; vertical pulls bias lats; extensions hit erectors (optional — they grow from rows/squats/deadlifts anyway).
- Retract scapulae ("pinch a pencil"), protract at bottom. Elbows tucked → lats; flared → upper back. Straps when grip fails first. Lengthened partials after failure (poor resistance profiles on rows).
- Honest caveat from FHP: direct hypertrophy research on back is thin — conclusions lean on anatomy + EMG.

## Quadriceps
- Three vasti (knee extension only) + rectus femoris (also hip flexor — bi-articular escape artist again).
- Squat patterns cover vasti; **leg extensions are SUPERIOR for rectus femoris** (study: Smith squat vs extensions — lateralis equal, rectus femoris substantially better from extensions).
- Lean the leg-extension backrest to ~40° hips for rectus femoris; use max-stretch range.
- Squat depth mainly buys joint-friendliness (growth similar at partial depths in cited studies); upright torso/narrow stance = quad-biased.
- Leg-extension PRE-FATIGUE before squats gave equal hypertrophy at lighter loads — tool for sparing the spine.

## Chest
- Pec major: clavicular (upper) + sternal (mid/lower). Pec minor ignored.
- Presses (flat/incline/decline/machine/dips/push-ups) + flies. **2–4 exercises is sufficient variety.**
- Angle biases: decline/high-to-low fly → lower; flat → all-rounder; ~30° incline maximizes upper-chest (steeper shifts to front delts).
- Surprising study: incline-ONLY pressing grew ALL three regions most — FHP still recommends angle variety pending replication.
- **Order finding: bench BEFORE triceps/shoulder isolation produced greater pec gains.**
- Moderate grip slightly wider than shoulders; deviate only for comfort or mind-muscle.

## Cross-muscle counting rules (for GymLog v2 volume dashboard)

| Muscle | Indirect rule |
|---|---|
| Triceps | each press set ≈ 0.5 set |
| Biceps | each back set ≈ 0.5 set |
| Delts | start direct at ~half of chest/back volume |
| Rear delts | rows count substantially |
| Erectors | grow free from hinges/squats |
| Abs/calves/forearms | compounds contribute little — direct work needed |

## Chest (051)

Three movement types needed to cover all fiber directions: **flat/decline press** (mid/lower), **incline press** (upper), **fly variation** (greatest ROM). Technique gold:
- Never bounce off the chest — the stretch-shortening cycle lives in the TENDON and robs tension exactly where growth lives (the stretched position).
- Scapula retraction on eccentric fine; don't force retraction during the press — pecs assist protraction, fighting yourself cuts activation.
- Stretch-under-load has independent hypertrophy evidence.

## Back (052)

Two categories: **horizontal rows** (mid/upper traps, rhomboids, rear delts, erectors) + **vertical pulls** (lats via bigger ROM); optional unilateral variants + shrugs.
- Actively retract scapulae on the pull, let them protract under stretch — traps attach to the scapula, not the arm.
- Pulling has DESCENDING strength curves (hardest when stretched): pick loads controllable at full contraction, or use ascending-curve machines (T-bar/landmine).
- Shrugs redundant if upright rows/OHP exist — most upper-trap fibers run horizontal.

## Quads (053)

Squat pattern (back squat/leg press/hack squat) PLUS leg extensions — rectus femoris is shortened at the hip during squats and escapes tension.
- Leg press: feet low on plate, full contact → more knee ROM.
- Squat: high-bar, narrower stance, upright torso biases quads.
- No bounce from the bottom (tendon reflex skips the loaded stretch); 0–3 RIR but strict — fatigue shifts tension to hips via forward lean.

## Glutes & Hamstrings (054)

Five tool families, pick by goal: hip hinge (SLDL/good morning/back extension — trains everything, best all-rounder) · squat pattern (glute-max biased, hams suppressed) · hip thrust (glutes-only, peak tension at lockout) · leg curls (hams only) · abduction machine (glute medius).
- Hinge rule: neutral/slightly-extended spine, rigid throughout — rounding + posterior pelvic tilt shortens hams and hands tension to the low back.
- Why squats/thrusts miss hams: simultaneous knee+hip extension creates opposing actions on this bi-articular muscle.

## Triceps (056)

Isolated elbow extensions (pushdowns, skull crushers, overhead cable extensions) are the ONLY category hitting all three heads; presses cover short heads mainly; pullovers long head only.
- Elbow-only motion: any shoulder movement during skull crushers steals long-head tension; slight shoulder extension drift on pushdowns is acceptable.
- Anatomy note: long head (scapula origin) + lateral head drive visible size; medial head hides beneath.

## Shoulders/Delts (057)

Presses (anterior), pulls (posterior), abduction (lateral) — that's the whole map. Front raises and reverse flies are unnecessary unless delts badly lag; add press/pull volume instead.
- Delts get heavy INDIRECT volume from all upper-body work — direct work may be optional for many lifters.
- Lateral raises: palms DOWN throughout — humeral external rotation (palms forward) leaks tension to front delts.
- Rotator cuff too small/deep to matter visually.

## Calves (058)

Standing calf raise = king (trains gastroc + soleus fully); seated raise = soleus-biased (knee flexion slackens gastroc).
- NO bouncing — Achilles tendon's stretch-shortening cycle is so strong it steals tension from the bellies entirely.
- ≥half the foot on the platform edge, drive through the ball of the foot (toe-only lets foot muscles fail first).
- Toe direction targets heads: out→medial, in→lateral, straight→balanced. Slight toe-out usually wins (medial head more visible).

# Flow High Performance — Injury Prevention & Load Management (Study Notes)

Source: Najib's saved playlist (13 entries; #13 "Stretching" was a duplicate already covered in [../fhp-recovery/NOTES.md](../fhp-recovery/NOTES.md) — 12 new videos processed). Raw transcripts in [transcripts/](transcripts/). Companions: [../rp-series/NOTES.md](../rp-series/NOTES.md) · [../rp-specialization/NOTES.md](../rp-specialization/NOTES.md) · [../fhp-periodization/NOTES.md](../fhp-periodization/NOTES.md) · [../fhp-recovery/NOTES.md](../fhp-recovery/NOTES.md).

## 1. The master principle: spikes injure, load protects

- **Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR)** [01][09]: this week's load ÷ your rolling 4-week weekly average. Spikes above the average = elevated injury risk; smooth gradual progressions keep it low. Units are flexible — for lifters, **sets per muscle per week** (or volume-load) works.
- Load itself is protective: high chronic workload shields you from competition/activity spikes; strength training cuts injury risk ~70%; proprioception training up to ~50%; stretching ≈ nothing [03].
- Practical ramp rules [09]: +1–2 sets/week maximum; after any layoff, ramp back over weeks; new exercises start at LOWER volume than what they replace, building over 1–2 weeks; before attempting true 1RMs, re-acclimate to heavy loads gradually if you've lived in 6–15 reps.

## 2. Training around pain — the priority ladder [10]

Pain ≠ damage (87.6% of pain-free adults have disc bulging on MRI). Hypertrophy is non-specific — almost always a pain-free path exists. Exhaust in this order:

1. **Zero cost**: swap the exercise · drop load below the pain threshold (e.g., stay under ~70% 1RM) · slow the tempo (any rep duration up to ~8s grows equally).
2. **Tiny cost**: shorten rests · tweak technique · BFR on limbs.
3. **Real cost**: limit ROM (full ROM is better — regain it later) · stop short of failure · cut volume (dose-response exists).

## 3. Blood Flow Restriction (BFR) [02]

- Cuffs/bands make light loads feel heavy physiologically (~50% capacity with cuff ≈ ~80% without).
- Main use = keeping muscle during joint flare-ups/rehab, not for healthy trainees. Protocol example: knee pain above 60% 1RM → stay ≤60%, near failure, add BFR → productive stimulus without irritating the joint.

## 4. Case-study playbook (what actually fixed injuries)

- **Tendon-type knee pain from lifting** [05]: remove bounce/stretch-shortening reps → pause squats & power variations; fix with eccentric-overload leg extensions (up heavy with 2 legs, lower slow on ONE leg 3–5 s) — relief within week one. Knee sleeves = symptom masker with dependency.
- **Compressive low-back pain** [06]: cap barbell work ~70%, pivot to horizontal loading (leg press, hip thrusts, back extensions, planks, ab wheel), partial pulls floor-to-knee, +5–10% every 4–5 weeks. Failed experiments worth knowing: self-massage, "functional core drills," breathing rituals.
- **Injury as a forced specialization block** [04]: keep training everything pain-free (often becomes a hypertrophy phase for non-injured areas), return to aggravating lifts gradually (~+0.5–1 km/wk style ramps). Never push through sharp pain and never take total rest then jump to "100%".

## 5. Hamstring resilience (bonus for anyone who runs/plays) [07] [08]

- Nordic curls cut hamstring strains up to ~51% — or machine curl single-leg eccentrics as an alternative.
- Sprint training also builds longer fascicles and speed, but Nordics carry the direct injury-rate evidence. Do both if you sprint; Nordics regardless.

## 6. Range of motion — final word [12] (see also recovery notes #11)

- Full ROM ≥ partials overall — but position matters more than completeness: **lengthened-position partials BEAT full ROM**, shortened partials are worst (calf studies within-subject).
- Strength follows specificity (partial squats improve partial 1RM; deep squats improve full 1RM + size).
- Train pain-free ranges first; treat stretch-position emphasis as the default growth driver everywhere (matches RP's lengthened-partial enthusiasm).

## 7. Goals cheat-sheet [11]

| Goal | Core prescription |
|---|---|
| Hypertrophy | target muscle limits set · 5–20 reps · ≤3 RIR · >10 sets/muscle/wk |
| Fat loss | deficit · lose ≤~1% BW/week · protein ≥1.5 g/kg · ~10k steps/day |
| Strength | specificity of the lift · plateau → grow prime movers · transition slowly |
| Health | lift 2–3×/wk 45–60 min · cardio 2–3×/wk (1–2 hard) |

# FHP Load Management Series (Study Notes)

Source: Najib's playlist #8 (5 entries; **3 were duplicates** already covered in [../fhp-injury/NOTES.md](../fhp-injury/NOTES.md) — ACWR, hamstring prevention, load-management principles). Only 2 new videos processed. Raw transcripts in [transcripts/](transcripts/).

## Video 2 — Volume landmarks & how they move

The adaptation curve vs training load has four zones:

1. Below minimum adaptive threshold → nothing happens (detraining).
2. **Optimal adaptive zone** → disrupt homeostasis + recover = growth. Live here.
3. Maximum recoverable volume (MRV) → ceiling of zone 2.
4. Beyond MRV → overreaching (short-term, recoverable) → chronically: overtraining.

**Key insight: every threshold shifts UP with experience.** A beginner grows from almost anything (wide adaptive zone); an advanced lifter needs far more load just to disrupt homeostasis. Practical consequence: your MEV/MRV numbers from a year ago are obsolete — re-probe them as you advance.

## Video 3 — Managing load across time horizons

- **Per session**: diminishing returns — doubling in-session load does NOT double adaptation; early sets carry most of the stimulus.
- **ACWR refined**: avoid spikes in BOTH directions — peaks AND sudden low points are both shocks. Gradual smooth trends beat jagged ones even when averages match.
- **Chronic load sweet spot**: too-low chronic load makes every hard event a spike (injury risk); too-high chronic load slowly breaks you. The winner is athlete C: moderately-high, steadily rising.
- **Microcycle undulation**: schedule at least one session ABOVE competition/event demand so events become non-spikes; lightest day right before the big one; active recovery (not zero) after it.
- **Annual balancing act**: fitness decays slowly, fatigue fast — so brief planned unload weeks buy big performance spikes cheaply, but don't linger in low-load phases or fitness drains and injury risk creeps back.

## New takeaways beyond previous notes

1. Thresholds are **personal and moving targets** — re-test your volume tolerance periodically (supports GymLog v2's "re-probe MEV/MRV" idea).
2. Spikes downward matter too — consistency itself is protective (GymLog streak tracking gets scientific justification).
3. "One session per week harder than your hardest event" — for Najib's trial: make one weekly session deliberately denser than any real-life physical demand.

## How this feeds GymLog

- Streak/consistency metric displayed as a *protective* factor, not gamification fluff.

# FHP Long-Term Periodization Collection (Study Notes)

Source: Najib's playlist #10 (42 entries — **28 duplicates** already processed across earlier series). 9 new videos downloaded; Olympic-weightlifting one (#10) has no subtitles; #08 (plyometric periodization) is sport-specific and skimmed only. Raw transcripts in [transcripts/](transcripts/).

## 1. Phases refresher with explicit trade-offs [01]

Accumulation / intensification / realization restated cleanly:
- Accumulation builds structure + work capacity + tissue resilience AND unloads joints vs constant heavy training — cost: performance dips under high fatigue.
- Intensification converts to specific performance — cost: no capacity building, joint risk if prolonged.
- Realization reveals built potential (fatigue ↓ + specificity ↑) — creates nothing; run long = detraining.
- Team-sport vs individual planning differs only in peak count: individuals peak on dates; seasons demand "good all season, best at finals."

## 2. Vertical Integration vs Block Periodization [07] [19] — the new model

Block periodization (endurance block → hypertrophy → strength → power → speed) fails because:
1. **Adaptation takes months-years** — one block per quality is too short to get good at anything.
2. **Gains decay** — by block 3, block-1 adaptations have atrophied.
3. Athletes must stay reasonably ready **year-round** (qualifiers, weekly fixtures).
4. Every block transition is a **sudden novel stimulus** → injury-risk spikes.

**Vertical integration**: train ALL relevant qualities simultaneously, always, shifting *emphasis* gradually toward the peak date. Nobody is ever out of shape; variation still exists (within-quality progression), so plateaus don't form.

For Najib: bodybuilding IS already vertical integration — hypertrophy every week forever, with emphasis knobs (specialization muscles, strength maintenance) turned up/down. This validates GymLog v2's planned design: a permanent hypertrophy engine + optional secondary-goal overlays.

## 3. Long-term strength progress = size first [22]

Strength expression = neural efficiency × prime-mover size:
- Neural efficiency adapts FAST (weeks) then plateaus hard — practicing 1RMs alone stops working.
- Muscle size raises the ceiling that efficiency expresses.

The 6-mesocycle peak template (for a given lift):
- Mesos 1–3: hypertrophy emphasis — 8–10 weekly sets, 6–12 reps (+6 accessory sets for prime movers).
- Meso 4: 5–8 sets, 3–5 reps (accessories unchanged).
- Meso 5: 4–7 sets, 2–4 reps.
- Meso 6: 3–6 sets, 1–3 reps, accessories trimmed — peak state, temporary by design.

## 4. Modern definition of periodization [37]

Periodization ≠ a specific block system; it just means **long-term management of training variables**. Then the punchline (echoing video 04 of the overload series):
- **Strength**: performance outcome → genuine periodization exists. Early cycle = hypertrophy emphasis (lift VARIATIONS, 8–12 reps, more accessories) → late cycle = competition lifts exactly as contested, 1–3 reps, low volume, fresh states.
- **Hypertrophy**: structural adaptation, no peak date → **no traditional periodization needed**. Change things reactively based on look/feel/progress, always chasing the best stimulus.

## 5. Balancing multiple goals [40]

- Practical constraints (time/effort realistically available) set the total budget FIRST; priorities allocate it.
- Diminishing returns per goal: early hours buy most of the progress.
- Compatibility: speed/power/strength/hypertrophy are mutually friendly. **Endurance mildly interferes** with lower-body strength/hypertrophy/power — interference grows with endurance days/week and session length; lifting doesn't hurt endurance at all.
- Systemic fatigue budgeting: heavy free-weight compounds + max-effort cardio + competition days all draw from one pool — if one rises, others should soften.
- Session order rule: skill → speed/power → max strength → hypertrophy → endurance (fatigue-sensitivity ordering; hypertrophy barely cares about fatigue).
- Seasonal shifting example: in-season = 2 lifts/week low-fatigue exercises, legs minimized; off-season = 4×/week full volume.

## 6. How often to change your routine? [42]

- **Keep each exercise ≥~6 weeks** — changing every workout measurably reduced quad growth vs consistency.
- BUT within a week, **multiple variants per muscle slightly beat a single exercise** — variety and consistency are different variables!
- Variety payoff depends on anatomy: chest/back (multi-function fibers) benefit from variety; biceps/calves (uniform fibers) barely.
- Legit reasons to change volume over a career: growth slowing (up-to-50% volume increases beat decreases for arm growth), lifestyle constraints (a business owner's honest capacity!), joint stress (fix technique → raise rep range → swap exercise, in that order), motivation/novelty (small but real), goal shifts across life stages.
- Volume-diminishing returns point cited: ~20+ sets/muscle/week.

## Cheat sheet

1. Hypertrophy has no peaking calendar — manage it reactively, forever.
2. If you ever chase a strength number: 3 hypertrophy mesos → taper reps/volume into a 1–3-rep peak.
3. Never rotate exercises faster than ~6 weeks; keep 2–3 variants per big-muscle group within each week.
4. One variable at a time; log WHY you changed something.
5. Endurance is allowed — just expect a slight tax on leg gains if it's frequent/long.
6. Order sessions: fresh stuff first, hypertrophy tolerates fatigue, endurance last.

# FHP Progressive Overload & Specificity Fundamentals (Study Notes)

Source: Najib's playlist #9 (6 entries; **2 duplicates** already in [../fhp-periodization/NOTES.md](../fhp-periodization/NOTES.md)). 4 new videos processed. Raw transcripts in [transcripts/](transcripts/).

## Video 1 — The Principle of Progressive Overload

- Overload disrupts homeostasis → adaptation. Same training forever = same body forever (Milo and the growing calf).
- Two progression families:
  - **Accumulation** (volume ↑, intensity flat): builds structure — muscle size, connective tissue, work capacity, tolerance for later hard phases.
  - **Intensification** (intensity ↑, volume flat): builds neural qualities.
- Week-to-week templates shown for every quality type: either sets ramp at fixed load (accumulation) or load ramps at fixed sets (intensification).
- Meso-to-meso shape: each block peaks slightly above the last, deload between — a rising staircase disguised as waves.

## Video 2 — Specificity & Transfer

- You get better at what you practice; everything else transfers only partially: back squat +10% might give front squat +5%, vertical jump +2.5%, sprint +1%.
- Diminishing returns hit the competition lift itself within months → general work keeps progress alive, BUT it must still *positively transfer*. Neutral work wastes time; extensive endurance can even NEGATIVELY transfer for power athletes.
- The pyramid: big general base × successful transfer = highest peak. General volume high far from peak; specific volume high near it.

## Video 4 — Specificity: strength vs hypertrophy (the liberating one)

- **Strength is a performance outcome**: expression depends on neural efficiency, which is EXERCISE-SPECIFIC. To peak a squat 1RM you must squat heavy — leg presses grow the engine but don't teach the car.
- **Hypertrophy is a structural adaptation**, not a performance outcome → the specificity principle barely applies! Any effective stimulus grows muscle; there's no "specific" biceps size to peak on a date.
- Implication: bodybuilders never need peaking cycles — adjust training by *perceived effectiveness*, not calendars. Strength athletes build mass generally, then sharpen specificity near test time.

## Video 6 — How close to failure? (the definitive answer)

**True failure = attempted-and-failed rep + maximal intent + zero technique drift.** "Your last rep should look like your first, only slower."

Evidence:
- Meta-analysis: tiny non-significant edge to failure training — EXCEPT in **trained lifters**, where it reached significance (their adaptation threshold demands more disruption).
- Velocity-loss study: growth improves as sets approach failure; beyond ~25% velocity loss, little extra.
- Load interaction (leg extension study, 80% vs 30% 1RM, one leg to failure / other not):
  - Heavy loads: failure irrelevant — all fibers recruited by load itself.
  - Light loads: failure REQUIRED — without it, low-threshold fibers do all the work (Henneman's size principle).

**The practical matrix:**

| Train FURTHER (~3–4 RIR) when… | Train CLOSER (0–1 RIR) when… |
|---|---|
| Heavy sets (<~10 reps) | Lighter sets (>10 reps) |
| Exercise early in session | Exercise late in session |
| Novice lifter | Advanced lifter |
| Brand-new exercise (excessive soreness risk) | Well-acquainted exercise |

Failure's hidden costs (why not always to failure):
1. **Within-session**: early failure sets bleed performance from later overlapping exercises (bench-to-failure wrecks your flies/triceps after).
2. **Between sessions**: extra damage/soreness inhibits subsequent days — matters for consecutive-day muscle training and novel lifts.
3. **Systemic**: chronic all-failure training trended cortisol ↑ testosterone ↓ across a program.

## Cheat sheet additions

- Bodybuilders live free of peaking pressure — pick stimuli by what's working, not dates.
- Default effort: compounds 2–4 RIR, isolations 0–2 (consistent with earlier series); push isolations/light sets to 0–1 as you advance.
- Sequence rule within sessions: hardest-to-recover compound first at moderate proximity; save failure for the tail.

## How this feeds GymLog (analysis-only)

- v2's RIR field now has a complete decision table (the matrix above) — implementable as automatic per-set suggestions based on exercise position, load zone, novelty, and user experience level.
- Novel-exercise guard: first 2 weeks with any new exercise, app caps suggested proximity at ~3 RIR.
- No new structures beyond previous mappings — this series mostly sharpens parameters.

# FHP Fundamentals of Periodization (Study Notes)

Source: Najib's playlist #6 (5 videos; "Planning the Mesocycle" was a duplicate already covered in [../fhp-periodization/NOTES.md](../fhp-periodization/NOTES.md) as video 17). Raw transcripts in [transcripts/](transcripts/). This series is the *skeleton overview* — read [../fhp-periodization/NOTES.md](../fhp-periodization/NOTES.md) for the muscle.

## The four-level planning pyramid [01]

| Level | Timeframe | Decides | Analogy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual plan | 52 weeks | Direction + key dates | Compass |
| Phases | months | Sequence toward peaks | Map |
| Mesocycle | 3–6 weeks | Overload + deload | Route for next stretch |
| Microcycle | 1 week | Exercises, sets, reps, loads | Actually walking |

Key discipline: **each level only holds what it can know**. The annual plan never contains exact weights/reps — plans change (injury, travel), so exact prescriptions are written only in the near term. Plan broad far away, detailed close up.

## Phase potentiation [03]

Sequence phases so each builds on the last:

- **Accumulation**: volume ↑, intensity flat → work capacity + tolerance for later phases.
- **Intensification**: volume held, load ↑ → converts tissue into performance.
- **Realization**: volume ↓↓, intensity ↑↑ → sharpest expression; usable only sparingly — too many realization phases and there's no accumulated base to express.
- For a bodybuilder: life happens in accumulation; the other two exist when a date demands expressed strength (or not at all).

## Planning the week (microcycle) [05]

- **Pair similar qualities on the same day** (max-speed with fast-power; upper strength with upper endurance) to avoid interference effects between days.
- Rest day goes AFTER the week's most intense session; active recovery there beats low-quality training.
- Before an important event: least fatiguing work only.
- When external demand rises (in-season equivalent), cut volume first, keep quality — train "as much as possible without overreaching," which shrinks as life stress grows.

## New takeaways beyond the periodization series

1. The **compass→map→route→walking** mental model for who-plans-what-at-which-horizon.
2. **Anti-overplanning rule**: never write exact numbers more than ~a block ahead.
3. Quality-pairing within training days to dodge interference (for GymLog's future: cardio placement advice).
4. Explicit framing that deload frequency should scale with stress: high-CNS work → shorter blocks.

## How this feeds GymLog (analysis-only)

- Confirms v2's structure: app = microcycle executor (weeks/exercises/sets), user picks phase intent per block (build/maintain/resensitize from earlier notes), annual view stays lightweight (goals + planned breaks), no fake long-term precision.
- Future "week review" screen mirrors the pyramid: did the week serve the block? did the block serve the phase?

# Flow High Performance — Programming & Periodization Series (Study Notes)

Source: Najib's saved playlist (Flow High Performance channel, evidence-based). Playlist had **92 entries**; ~45 sport-specific videos (soccer/basketball/sprint/power/throwing) were skipped as out of scope for bodybuilding; **41 hypertrophy/strength-programming videos processed**. Video 27 has no subtitles; video 83's transcript was corrupted. Raw transcripts: [transcripts/](transcripts/) (numbered by original playlist position).

Companions: [../rp-series/NOTES.md](../rp-series/NOTES.md) (mesocycle mechanics) · [../rp-specialization/NOTES.md](../rp-specialization/NOTES.md) (muscle specialization). Where FHP disagrees with RP, both positions are shown — they converge more than they differ.

## 1. The foundations [19] [17] [02]

- **Periodization** = manipulating *volume* (how much) and *intensity* (how heavy/how fast) to manage the **fitness–fatigue model**: performance = fitness − fatigue. Fatigue spikes fast and decays fast; fitness builds slow and decays slow.
- A **mesocycle** = overload weeks + deload. Three flavors, sequenced across a year:
  - **Accumulation**: volume ↑, intensity flat → builds muscle + work capacity.
  - **Intensification**: load ↑ (~+2.5 kg/wk), volume flat → converts size to neural strength.
  - **Realization**: volume ↓↓, load ↑↑ → peaking; ends in a *taper* (goal: peak performance), not a deload (goal: just clear fatigue).
- **A bodybuilder lives almost entirely in accumulation.** Intensification/realization exist to express strength on demand, not to grow.
- Deloads are not lost time: fitness persists while masking fatigue clears — that's why 3-on/1-off beats grinding forever.

## 2. Building a hypertrophy program [92] [23] [78] [75]

Build order (video 92): frequency → split → exercises → proximity to failure → reps/load → rest → sets → progression.

- **Volume is king**: ~10–20 hard sets/muscle/week large muscles, up to 20–30 small muscles [23]; find your dose by trial. Weekly sets/muscle is THE unit of programming.
- **Frequency ≥2×/muscle/week** slightly better; it's a vehicle for volume, not a driver [78]. No extra benefit ≥3×.
- **Split**: no best split — total weekly volume decides growth. Bro-splits concentrate a muscle's whole week into one brutal session = hardest way to accumulate quality volume. PPL/upper-lower/full-body all fine at equal volume [78].
- **Exercises**: compounds first, isolation last; ≥2 variants per priority muscle covering different vectors (flat+incline+cable fly; vertical pull+row) [38][92]. Max ~5 sets per exercise per session, then add another exercise [23].
- **Reps**: practical band 5–20 (anything 5–30 works near failure); compounds low end, isolations high end [23][92].
- **Rest**: 1–3 min (≥2 min max stimulus; ≤90 s acceptable when time-poor).
- Beginners [75]: 3 full-body days, machines first (stability demand lowest), ~3 sets/exercise, compounds 6–12 isolations 12–20, log every session. Consistency beats optimization in year one.

## 3. Effort: RIR / RPE / autoregulation [79] [86] [22] [26] [82] [33]

- **RPE 10 = failure = 0 RIR**; each point below ≈ 1 rep in reserve. Trained lifters judge within ~1 rep near failure but err wildly far from it [86].
- Prescribe **RIR ranges**, not exact reps:
  - Heavy/stable-demanding compounds (squat, SLDL): **2–4 RIR**
  - Easy compounds (rows, machine presses): **1–3 RIR**
  - Isolations: **0–2 RIR** [82]
  - By rep zone: 5–10-rep sets → 2–4 RIR; 10–15 → 1–3; 15+ → 0–2 [82]
- Don't train farther than ~RPE 6 (≈RIR 4): beyond that, high-threshold fibers never get recruited — junk sets [26].
- **Autoregulation patterns**:
  - Fixed load + fixed sets, ramp RPE 6→7→8→9 weekly → reps climb automatically (the default hypertrophy meso) [22][26]
  - Reactive deloads: deload when logged reps plateau/decline, not on a calendar date [33]
- Fatigue naturally drops reps across sets at constant weight/RIR (9→8→7) — normal, not a form problem [79].

## 4. Progression — week-to-week [13] [48] [77] [88] [90]

Pick **ONE lever per mesocycle**, freeze the rest:

| Lever | Protocol |
|---|---|
| More reps | Same sets+load; beat last week's TOTAL reps by ~1–3/exercise |
| More sets | +1 set/muscle/week for ~3 weeks, then deload |
| Closer to failure | W1 RIR 4/3 (compound/isolation) → W4 RIR 0–1 |
| RPE ramp | RPE 6→7→8→9 weekly, loads fixed |

- **Static vs ramped weekly volume produce equal growth** when total meso volume matches — static wins on simplicity [87]. (RP prefers ramping toward MRV; both work — choose one system.)
- Overload can't be scheduled: adaptation speed is individual. Train hard at fixed RIR with strict technique and let reps rise as an *output* of growth [77][88].
- Never buy reps with shortened ROM or momentum: lengthened full-ROM beats partials even with lighter loads; controlled eccentrics; order changes strength expression, not hypertrophy [90].
- Double progression template: e.g., 50 kg ×8/7/6 → build reps to 11 → jump to 55 kg, repeat [92].

## 5. Progression — block-to-block (meso-to-meso) [40] [50] [32] [26]

Escalation hierarchy per exercise (try in this order, one change at a time):

1. **Change nothing** — fresh adaptation still yields more reps (works 1–2 mesos).
2. **Small load bump** (+~5%) keeping reps ≥~6 compound / ≥8 isolation.
3. **Swap the exercise** only if stalled 2+ blocks or joints hurt; keep its replacement 3–6 mesos; keep compounds ≥3 consecutive blocks otherwise [10].
4. **Add +1–2 weekly sets** — last resort, max 1–2 muscles above baseline at once.
5. When reps average <6 (compound), reset into a lighter/higher-rep variant [23][26].

- **Resensitization**: after long high-volume stretches (or every 8–12 months), run ~50% volume briefly so muscle re-becomes responsive to volume [32][36].
- Keep exercises/reps stable inside a block; novelty is imposed by NEED, not boredom.

## 6. Volume landmarks (RP ∩ FHP) [10] [36]

Per muscle per week:

| Landmark | Range |
|---|---|
| Maintenance | ~6–10 sets |
| Minimum effective volume (MEV) | ~8–12 sets |
| Maximum adaptive volume | ~18–30 sets (individual, floating) |
| Deload | ~⅓–⅔ of normal |

- Never jump volume suddenly (connective tissue lags); +~2 sets/meso for prioritized muscles [36].
- Prioritize ≤2 lagging muscles per phase; everyone else sits at maintenance — then resensitize [36].

## 7. Strength-specific models (context for bodybuilders)

- Strength = neural efficiency × prime-mover size. The annual sequence: **accumulation → intensification → realization**, peaking only 1–3×/year [02][49][24].
- Plateau on a big lift? It usually means neural efficiency is maxed relative to muscle: run a hypertrophy block (higher reps, accessories), then re-peak [85].
- Back-off sets: 1 heavy top set + lighter back-offs (~−20%) supplies skill practice + volume without joint wear [58].
- Even strength programs are mostly hypertrophy blocks wearing a costume — muscle is the long-term strength currency [73].

## Cheat sheet — the consensus rules

1. Program **sets/muscle/week** (10–20 typical) and hit each muscle ≥2×/week.
2. Most working sets land **1–3 RIR**; isolations may touch 0–1; nothing beyond RPE 6 distance.
3. Reps anywhere in **5–20** near failure; compounds low, isolations high.
4. Pick ONE progression lever per meso; freeze everything else.
5. Block = 3–5 overload weeks + deload (~half-to-two-thirds volume).
6. Between blocks escalate per exercise: nothing → +load → swap → +sets.
7. Log every set — your logbook IS the progress record and the deload trigger.
8. Resensitize with low-volume phases when everything stalls; specialize ≤2 muscles at once.
9. Strict technique, lengthened ROM, controlled eccentric — stimulus outranks numbers.
10. Bodybuilders live in accumulation; peak strength only when a date demands it.

# FHP Practical Applications of Science (Study Notes)

Source: Najib's playlist #7 (10 entries; "Sprinting vs Nordics" was a duplicate already in [../fhp-injury/NOTES.md](../fhp-injury/NOTES.md)). Single-study breakdowns — each video = one paper + what it changes in practice. Raw transcripts in [transcripts/](transcripts/).

## Bodybuilding-critical studies

### Volume: dose-response is real (video 4)
Schoenfeld 2018 MSSE — 34 trained men, 8 weeks, 1 vs 3 vs 5 sets/exercise ×9 compounds ×3/week to failure:
- **Strength**: all groups improved ~equally (1-set group even edged bench) — strength rides on practice + intensity, not volume.
- **Hypertrophy**: clean dose-response on EVERY muscle measured — 5 sets > 3 sets > 1 set, no ceiling found yet.
- Takeaway: never judge a program by strength PRs alone; size follows weekly sets.

### Frequency barely matters at equated volume (video 6)
Grgic/Schoenfeld 2018 — trained men, same exercises+sets split across **3 vs 6 days/week**, 6 weeks:
- All muscle thicknesses and 1RMs grew the same; the lone biceps edge for 6×/week was likely noise.
- Confirms the periodization-series rule: program weekly volume; frequency is just distribution.

### Train biceps LONG (video 10)
Pedrosa 2020 — preacher curls trained only in stretched half (0–50° elbow) vs shortened top half (80–130°), 5 weeks:
- Stretched-position group grew significantly more overall, especially distally.
- Practical: full ROM always; prefer stretch-loaded variants (incline curls > preacher-short-range).

### Foam rolling = neural parlor trick (video 2)
2018 J Sports Sci — daily foam rolling vs daily eccentric calf work, 4 weeks:
- Foam roll: acute dorsiflexion spike, gone within 24h, baseline after 4 weeks.
- Eccentric training: slower but STRUCTURAL gains (sarcomeres added in series → lasting ROM).
- Takeaway: want permanent ROM? Load the stretch (eccentrics/full-ROM lifting). Rolling = temporary pre-session feel-better.

### Strength training as injury insurance (video 7)
Lauersen meta-analyses 2014 + 2018: stretching ≈ zero protection; **strength training −~70% injury risk**; proprioception −~50%. Mechanisms: load variation (vs sport repetition), tissue conditioning raising failure thresholds (e.g., eccentric hamstrings vs sprint strains), technique/coordination carryover, psychological readiness.

## Sport-specific ones (kept brief)

- **01 Plyometrics** (youth soccer): unilateral > bilateral plyos; combined best for speed/endurance. Marginal for lifters.
- **03 Sprint ground forces**: acceleration = horizontal propulsion; top speed = vertical force, short contacts; minimize braking via technique.
- **05 Power session 24h pre-match**: low-volume jump squats IMPROVED explosive performance next day (+5% CMJ at 24h) — priming beats total rest before an event; irrelevant to hypertrophy blocks.
- **08 Strength → sprinting**: heavy squats/RDL/Nordics (+25 kg squat in 6 wks!) improved 5–10m sprints but not max velocity (elastic-limited).

## Cross-series synthesis

This series' studies are the *evidence footnotes* behind rules already in your other notes:
- Dose-response volume → RP/FHP "sets per muscle per week" programming
- Frequency equivalence → "frequency is a vehicle" 
- Long-length superiority → lengthened-partials emphasis (RP specialization, ROM video)
- Strength-as-insurance → injury series hierarchy

# FHP Creating a Hypertrophy Training Program — The 8-Step Builder (Study Notes)

Source: Najib's playlist #14 (8 parts, ALL new). This is FHP's complete program-construction walkthrough — **the algorithmic spec for GymLog v2's program builder**, step by step, with their worked example running through every part. Raw transcripts in [transcripts/](transcripts/).

## Step 1 — Mesocycle skeleton (Part 1)

- Block of 3–6 weeks = overloading weeks + deload.
- Overloading = volume between a minimum adaptive threshold and your max recoverable threshold (proximity to failure assumed).
- **Deload = 50–65% of normal overloading volume** — still enough to maintain, low enough to shed fatigue.
- Ratio: **1 deload per 2–5 overloading weeks**. Advanced lifters sit at the frequent end (bigger stimulus → bigger fatigue); novices at the far end.
- Their worked example: 4-week meso, deload placed at WEEK ONE, three overloading weeks after. (FHP consistently front-loads the easy week.)

## Step 2 — Frequency & split (Part 2)

Two separate dials, neither affects growth much at equated volume:
- **Workout frequency** (days/week total) — lifestyle decision, not optimization.
- **Muscle-group frequency** — spreading a muscle's weekly sets over more sessions raises set QUALITY (less intra-session fatigue); unclear if that improves outcomes.
- Guidelines: hit each muscle **>1×/week** when possible; never high-volume same-muscle on consecutive days.
- Worked example: 4-day upper/lower, every muscle 2×/week non-consecutive.

## Step 3 — Exercise selection (Part 3)

Six filters, in priority order:

1. **Biomechanics** — does the lift load the target muscle AT ALL?
2. **Stimulus-to-fatigue ratio** — stimulus per unit of systemic cost.
3. **Range of motion** — bigger ROM generally wins.
4. **Compound vs isolation** — compounds are efficient but only ONE muscle limits them; isolation always makes the target the limiter. Bi-articular muscles (biceps during rows/pulls) escape compound tension → need direct work. Rule: majority of volume from compounds, isolation fills coverage gaps.
5. **Tension curve** — prefer constant-tension (cable fly > dumbbell fly) or hard-at-stretch profiles (T-bar/landmine rows match the descending pull curve).
6. **Individual anatomy + preference** — pain-free beats theoretically-optimal; enjoyment raises effort.

Session-design rule from their build: **never stack multiple heavy compounds in one session** (shared stabilizer fatigue + impractical rest periods). Day emphases alternate (day 1 press-biased, day 3 pull-biased).

Their example program (steal for defaults):
- D1 (chest/triceps/front-delt bias): incline BB press, seated cable row, DB OHP, cable fly, pushdown, EZ curl
- D2 (quad bias): back squat, back extension, leg extension, standing calf raise
- D3 (back/rear-delt/biceps bias): barbell row, incline BB press, lat pulldown, reverse fly, EZ curl, pushdown
- D4 (glute/ham bias): stiff-leg deadlift, leg press, leg curl, standing calf raise

## Step 4 — Volume (Part 4)

- Metric = **sets per muscle per week** (rep-range equivalence makes sets the honest currency).
- Dose-response confirmed, ceiling unknown — but you'll hit a practical limiter FIRST, in this order: **joint tolerance → systemic fatigue → time**.
- So: perform the most volume you can handle *without joint pain, without breaching systemic capacity, within your real schedule* — then allocate by PRIORITY (weak points get more).
- Their balanced example: chest 12 / back 12 / quads 9 / hams 9 / shoulders 8 direct / biceps 8 direct / triceps 8 direct / calves 6 direct.
- Deload weeks run at **~60–65%** of these numbers.

## Step 5 — Proximity to failure (Part 5)

- Global default: **0–3 RIR everywhere**; avoid habitual true failure (disproportionate fatigue vs marginal growth).
- Per category (their exact prescriptions):
  - Heavy lower-body compounds (squat, SLDL): **2–3 RIR**
  - Other compounds: **1–2 RIR**
  - Isolations: **0–1 RIR**
- By rep zone: 6–12-rep sets → 1–3 RIR; 12–20+-rep sets → 0–2 RIR.
- **RIR stays CONSTANT across the whole mesocycle** — progression is NOT pre-programmed (see Step 7). This is FHP's signature difference from RP's ramp-to-failure.

## Step 6 — Rep ranges & load (Part 6)

- Reps and load are ONE variable (inverse relationship) — prescribe NEITHER strictly.
- Band: **6–20 reps** equally valid near failure. Compounds suit 6–12 (cardio/stabilizers cap high-rep squats); isolations suit 12–20 (heavy single-joint loading is injurious).
- High-rep work goes LATE in sessions (its metabolic fatigue would tax following exercises).
- Leave reps/load cells EMPTY for the lifter to record and self-adjust mid-session — their worked example shows dropping load between sets to stay in the target zone while honoring RIR.
- Philosophy: rigid numbers can't match an individual's actual rate of progress.

## Step 7 — Progressive overload (Part 7)

- **Not written into the program at all.** Fixed sets + fixed RIR → performance moves on its own timeline.
- Novice: rep improvements visible within one meso. Advanced: may take 2–3 mesos before a trend appears.
- Judge performance ONLY in the 6–20 band, strict technique, across MONTHS — never compare single sessions.
- Overload = the natural result of growth expressing itself, not a schedule to force.

## Step 8 — Long-term periodization (Part 8)

Periodization for hypertrophy = **reactive management after each meso**, driven by four inputs:

| Input | Signals |
|---|---|
| Performance trend | improving in 6–20 band = working; stagnant/declining across MULTIPLE mesos = adjust |
| Systemic fatigue | lethargy, poor sleep, illness risk, effortful sessions |
| Joint pain | acute (one-off) vs chronic aches (volume/load too high for that joint) |
| Monotony | boredom threatening adherence |

Adjustment levers, in order:
1. **Change nothing** — if trending well, repeat the program!
2. **Volume ±**: increase for a stalled-but-healthy muscle (e.g., chest 12→14) or for more growth when recovering fine; decrease when systemically fried or a joint complains.
3. **Exercise swap**: compounds kept ≥3 mesos, isolations 1–2 mesos acceptable; swap triggers = plateau, monotony, pain — swap to a close variant (incline BB → flat DB press).

## The complete builder algorithm (GymLog v2 spec)

```
1. Meso length: 4 wks default (wk1 = deload @60–65%, wks 2–4 identical overload)
2. Split: upper/lower ×2 default; each muscle ≥2×/wk non-consecutive
3. Exercises: 6-filter selection; ≤1 heavy compound/session; day emphases alternate
4. Volume: sets/muscle/wk picker with practical-limit warnings (joints/systemic/time)
5. RIR: auto-assigned by category (squat/SLDL 2-3, compounds 1-2, isolation 0-1)
6. Reps/load: left blank; user logs; app suggests load adjustments to stay in zone
7. Progression: none forced — app charts rep trends per exercise
8. End-of-meso review screen: performance/fatigue/joints/monotony → recommend hold/volume±/swap
```

This 8-step flow + the template architecture (fhp-templates notes) + RP landmarks together define the entire v2 training engine.

# Flow High Performance — Recovery Series (Study Notes)

Source: Najib's saved playlist (13 videos, Flow High Performance). All processed; raw transcripts in [transcripts/](transcripts/). Companions: [../rp-series/NOTES.md](../rp-series/NOTES.md) · [../rp-specialization/NOTES.md](../rp-specialization/NOTES.md) · [../fhp-periodization/NOTES.md](../fhp-periodization/NOTES.md).

## 1. The recovery hierarchy [01] [05]

- Recovery = return to baseline performance. **Fundamentals drive ~90%**: workload management (stay between minimum adaptive threshold and maximum recoverable load ~95% of the time), adequate calories, protein spread over meals, carbs for training fuel, 7–9 h consistent sleep.
- Everything else — massage, stretching, ice baths, saunas, compression — adds **~5–10% combined**, mostly placebo.
- **Cold water immersion eases soreness but BLUNTS hypertrophy** — skip it after lifting; only sensible post-competition when next performance matters more than adaptation.
- Active recovery: light, unloaded, joint-friendly movement on rest days — must not itself create fatigue.

## 2. How long does recovery actually take? [02] [07] [10] [03]

Per-system timelines differ wildly:

| System | Recover/adapt |
|---|---|
| Technique/skill | hours — trainable daily |
| Muscle protein synthesis | trained: near baseline ~24h · untrained >48h |
| Lifting performance (bar velocity) | ~back to baseline by **48h** |
| Soreness | novel work: peaks ~48h, can linger >4 days · familiarized: ~24h |
| CNS-heavy work (max strength/speed) | up to weeks → cap at 1–2×/week |
| Tendons/ligaments | months (poor blood supply) |

- With weekly volume equated, hitting a muscle 3× vs 6×/week — even back-to-back days or twice per DAY — produced identical growth [07]. The "48–72h rule" is a starting heuristic, not physics.
- Defaults: ≥48h between same-muscle sessions; 3–4 days after high-volume/novel sessions; otherwise use biofeedback (performance first, slight-soreness okay once past its peak).
- Regional nuance: lats need 2–3 days after pull-up volume; mid-traps/rhomboids from rows recover overnight — rotate row/pull variations across adjacent days [07].
- Creatine kinase stays elevated up to 7 days post-workout and is irrelevant to outcomes — don't read blood panels as fatigue truth [10].

## 3. Fatigue: what helps growth vs what wastes it [12] [13]

- **Local fatigue of the target muscle IS the stimulus** — chase it via proximity to failure + sufficient volume.
- Global fatigue that buys NO growth: excessive cardiorespiratory drain, psychological grind, joint stress. Strip it.
- The "inverted-U" (more hard training eventually kills gains) is NOT supported by data: quads grew more on 52 sets/wk than 42 and 22; 32 weekly sets/muscle beat 24 and 16; post-failure partials beat stopping at failure [13].
- Real dangers are "too much too soon": sudden workload spikes (acute:chronic ratio predicts injury), extreme novel protocols (untrained 20×10 maximal eccentrics depressed strength >96h), rhabdomyolysis (dark urine = emergency).
- Machines grow muscle equally to free weights at lower systemic cost; cheat form doubles volume-load without doubling growth [12].
- Overtraining syndrome is vanishingly rare (~8 cases ever in resistance training) [08]. Spectrum: functional overreaching (days–2wks, supercompensation) → non-functional (2wks–2mo) → OTS (>2mo).
- Mental fatigue is real: a 30-minute cognitive task before training measurably cut total volume load [08].

## 4. Sleep & life stress [09]

- One bad night barely matters: max strength survives 24-h deprivation; caffeine restores multi-set workload.
- Chronic restriction is the killer: 5.5h × 14 nights while dieting = MORE lean lost, LESS fat lost; appetite hormones shift (~+250 kcal/day eaten); mood degrades reliably (154 studies).
- Sleep EXTENSION (+1–2h) or strategic naps give small measurable performance gains — the cheapest legal enhancement.
- Skill execution suffers most from poor sleep; max strength least.

## 5. Nutrition around training [04] [06] [01]

- Hierarchy for growth [06]: **adherence > effort (≤5 RIR + 10–20 sets/muscle/week) > calories/protein (≥1.5 g/kg/day; surplus > maintenance > deficit) > secondary variables > sleep/stress > supplements (creatine, protein powder, caffeine 3–6 mg/kg)**.
- Post-workout meal: low fat, high protein, moderate–high carbs incl. some fast carbs. Urgency depends on context — rush only if training again same day [04].
- Example recovery meal ≈25 g whey + milk + banana + rice cakes/jam [04].

## 6. Deloads & breaks (recovery view) [02] [03] [10]

- Deload every **3–6 weeks** (closer to 3 when intensity/failure work is high); novices can stretch blocks to 5–6 weeks.
- Take **1–2 multi-week full breaks per year** — your muscles recover faster than your tendons; connective tissue needs the annual vacation.
- Daily-max-squat case studies: lifters improved 1RMs training daily for 37 days — adaptation outruns fatigue when exposure is familiar and gradual [10][13].

## Cheat sheet

1. Nail sleep (7–9h consistent), food (protein ≥1.5g/kg, enough calories), workload discipline — that's 90%.
2. Retrain a muscle when PERFORMANCE returns (~48h default), not when soreness vanishes.
3. Never take ice baths during a growth phase.
4. Stretch only to unlock positions you can't reach; full-ROM lifting builds flexibility anyway.
5. Chase local muscle burn; refuse pointless global suffering (junk cardio-of-fatigue, ego joint stress).
6. "Too hard" almost doesn't exist — "too fast" does. Gradual ramps always.
7. Protect sleep like a training session; extension beats optimization gadgets.
8. Annual full breaks are part of the program, not a failure of it.

# Dr. Andy Galpin Performance Clips — Study Notes

Source: Najib's playlist #17 (46 titled videos; ~34 more were unavailable/deleted). Different channel from FHP — broader performance/health science. Raw transcripts in [transcripts/](transcripts/). Organized by theme.

## A. Training principles & programming

**Seven variables of any program [39]**: Choice, Order, Volume, Intensity, Frequency, Rest, Progression.
- Hypertrophy: order barely matters · protect weekly volume · rest 30s–5min all fine · don't hammer one muscle back-to-back days.
- Strength/power: ORDER is critical (hardest first) · intensity (%1RM) is king · volume capped by force recovery · same muscles can train daily (little local fatigue) · ≥3 min rests.

**Goal-specific numbers [40]**: hypertrophy 8–12 typical (~70–80% 1RM), anything 5–30+ at equal effort · strength 3–5 reps maximal · power ≤6 reps fast (load varies: small lifts 30–50% 1RM, squat 50–60%, Olympic 80–95%) · strength/power rest ≥3 min — beginners take MORE rest than feels necessary (you can't perceive the last ~8% recovery).

**Minimal viable growth [42]**: slight surplus · protein floor 1.6 g/kg (2.2 fine, adds little) · full ROM · 15–20 working sets/muscle/week · 2–3×/muscle/week · **"72-hour rule": stimulate each muscle to a good pump every ~72h without annihilating it**.

**Abs are just muscle [01][03][08][09]**: ~50/50 fiber split like quads — no daily-training exemption. Core week = 4 categories (flexion / rotation / anti-extension / anti-rotation), one each minimum. Progression ladder: isometric holds 45–90s → anti-movement high-rep → loaded eccentric → heavy concentric 5–8 reps ("curl one vertebra at a time"). Abs blueprint: 2–4×/week, 10–20 sets/muscle/week, 0–2 RIR — thickness gains 10–20% in trials; fix anterior pelvic tilt for instant leanness.

**Glutes (Contreras) [22][23]**: #1 mistake = insufficient effort, then masking with variety/volume. Log every lift and chase it. Glute template: 3×/week, each session 1 exercise from each family — thrust/bridge · squat/lunge · hinge · abduction ("rule of thirds" rotating CNS-heavy vertical moves, low-damage horizontal moves, penalty-free abduction). Deep > shallow squats for glute/adductor growth.

**Strength standards & testing [41]**: bench ≈1× BW men (0.6 women) · leg press ≈2× BW (1.5) · grip >45 kg men / >28 kg women; side asymmetry >10% = 2.67× sarcopenia risk. Grip strength predicts lifespan better than size — $30 dynamometer = annual health KPI.

## B. Recovery: sleep, stress, breath

**Sleep is the highest-leverage free drug [30][38][16]**: target 7.5–8.5h. Landmark trial — identical deficit, 5.5h vs 8.5h sleep → **55% less fat lost, ~60% less lean retained**. Chronic <5h/night = +251% type-2-diabetes hazard. After a bad night: double/triple protein, keep walking, nap 30–90min. Tips: caffeine cutoff ≥8h pre-bed · no novel/agitating content at night · remove clocks/sleep scores · bedroom CO₂ <900ppm, noise <35dB · snoring → mouth tape + nasal dilator or sleep study. Men vs women sleep gap is real but ~25–30 min — don't engineer anxiety over viral claims.

**Stress down-regulation — the 5 Ps [07]**: Perspective (mantra) · Place (quiet/dark) · Position (pelvis above heart) · Pace of breath (slow deep ~5 min/day sweet spot, extend exhales) · Palpation (soft-ball self-massage). Stack all five in one 5-minute session.

**Breathing mechanics [06][45]**: Zone 1 belly (relaxing) · Zone 2 ribs (everyday ideal) · Zone 3 face/neck gasping (emergency only — chronic = jaw pain, headaches). Rib-release ball drill 2 min/side. Respiratory rate tracks CO₂/pH, not oxygen — **each +1 breath/min = ×1.25 stress odds, independent of HRV**: log it as an overload early-warning.

**HRV composure training [12]**: ~10s breath cycle (4s in, 6s out) builds "coherence"; find your personal resonance with feedback; when spiked mid-event, change ONLY the breath.

## C. Monitoring & overtraining

**Personal baseline dashboard [35]**: track 1 objective marker (RHR/HRV/bodyweight) + 1 subjective (mood/desire) + 1 performance anchor, ~30 days untouched. Flag: >1 SD from mean for 3–5 days = attention; >2 SD for 2 days = act now. Ignore single bad days.
**Blood work as business KPIs [32]**: not required, but establishes your "passport" baselines early 20s; interpret against YOUR history not lab ranges ($500–700/panel).
**Fitness dashboard [44]**: resting HR 40s–50s bpm good (>60 warrants attention) · VO2 max: average 35–45 ml/kg/min, independence threshold ~18, elite 50+ — two free yearly numbers.

## D. Nutrition & supplement stack (evidence-ranked)

| Item | Protocol | Payoff |
|---|---|---|
| **Fish oil** [34][29] | 2–4 g EPA+DHA/day (brain ceiling ~2g); omega-3 index target 8–10% | **+14–15% RMR, +4% lean mass over 12 wks**, brain perfusion, inflammation |
| **Creatine** [25][36] | 5–10 g/day maintenance; 20–30 g/day for 7 days in high-risk windows | Brain ATP buffer — cognition under sleep deprivation; TBI protection evidence ~2/5 |
| **Magnesium** [28] | 400 mg/day (bisglycinate/malate/threonate) | 600+ reactions; drops after TBI; cheapest multiplier |
| Collagen [20] | 5 g/day × 12 months | ~4–7% BMD gain at spine/femoral neck |
| Protein pre-bed [05] | ~40 g, <220 kcal, ~30 min before sleep | Raises overnight MPS; doesn't blunt fat loss; 12-wk: higher CSA + 1RM |
| Water [29] | 0.5 L pre-meal | RMR bump + satiety-driven weight loss |

Myth-busts: calcium pills above dietary intake add nothing; spicy food ≈ 3–5 kcal; green-tea/thermo stacks transient (+7–15% RMR for hours).

## E. Metabolism & body composition

**TDEE decomposition [31]**: EAT ~10–20% (hard to exceed 30%) · NEAT 5–30% (often rivals exercise!) · TEF 8–15% · RMR 40–80%. Higher TDEE = eat more while leaning out — build NEAT habits + muscle.
**Sleep × composition bidirectional [30]**: more muscle improves sleep; bad sleep wrecks both.

## F. Brain, bones, longevity

**Exercise program for the brain [27]**: resistance 2×/wk (5–8 whole-body exercises, ~2 sets each) + Norwegian 4×4 intervals 1–2×/wk + easy cardio 1–3×/wk. Grip strength explains ~30% of Alzheimer's death risk variance; even 1 lift session/wk improves cognition.
**VO2 max = compounding asset [43][46]**: mix ~70% low-intensity + real HIIT (30/30 ×4–12 or 4-min all-out ×2–4, truly maximal) 2–3×/wk. Mortality falls 64→18.6 per 10k men from least→most fit quintile; benefit never plateaus. Escaping the bottom fitness quintile is the biggest single win.
**Bones respond to CHANGE in load [19][21]**: LIFTMOR trial — 2×/wk, 30 min, deadlift+squat+OHP 5×5 @85% 1RM + jump-chin-drop: zero fractures, superior BMD in fragile postmenopausal women. Active kids carry ~10% more BMD into their 30s. Lifting never stunted anyone's growth (1960s myth from child labor confounds).
**Brain fuel rules [24][26]**: homocysteine <11, omega-3 index ≥8%, vitamin D/iron/B12 checked; post-concussion brain goes glucose-resistant → ketones/lactate enter freely (Galpin's ketone-ester call after head trauma).

## G. Mind & discipline

- **Mental toughness [15]**: "try harder" is the only variable that always transfers; willingness under discomfort is built purely by repeated exposure.
- **Self-talk tool [10]**: address yourself in second person through an admired mentor's voice (Ethan Kross, *Chatter*).
- **Visualization [11]**: most research-backed mental skill — match mode to purpose (confidence/coping/familiarization/technique); ~10 min 1–2×/day, always include emotion; NOT right before bed.
- **Suffering as dopamine repair [14]**: comfort-seeking hollows reward sensitivity; disciplined struggle restores it.
- **Timing [37]**: perform hard efforts at your habitual circadian time — timing alone predicted ~3% of Olympic swim results.

# EP1 — Assessing & Improving Fitness

## The 9 fitness pillars
Every goal reduces to nine adaptations. "Fat loss" and "health" are **byproducts**, not training styles: your health protocol = whatever trains your weakest pillar.

1. **Skill/Technique** · 2. **Speed** · 3. **Power** (speed × force) · 4. **Force/Strength** (1RM — NOT reps) · 5. **Hypertrophy** (size) · 6. **Muscular endurance** (5–25/50-rep efforts, local not cardio) · 7. **Anaerobic capacity** (~30–45s all-out) · 8. **VO₂ max** (8–15 min peak) · 9. **Long duration** (20–60 min steady state)

## Testing battery & benchmarks

| Test | Benchmark |
|---|---|
| Resting HR | <60 bpm (75+ = something wrong) |
| Movement screen | Film press/pull/squat/hinge frontal+side; score joints: symmetry/stability/awareness/ROM; never train a 0 |
| Broad jump | ≥ your height (women −15%) |
| Vertical jump | ≥24″ (50+: ~20″; women −15%) |
| Grip dynamometer | Men ≥40 kg (good 60+), women ≥35 kg (good ~50); sides <10% apart |
| Dead hang | ≥30 s min, 60 s+ good |
| Leg extension 1RM | 1× bodyweight (−10%/decade after 40) |
| Goblet hold | ½ BW at bottom for 45 s |
| FFMI (DEXA) | Men ≥20, women ≥18; <17/<15 = urgent |
| Push-ups | Men ≥25, women ≥15, no pausing |
| Planks | Front 60 s / side 45 s |
| 75% test | 75% 1RM → >8 reps target |
| HR recovery | ~30 bpm drop/min ×3 min after all-out |
| Cooper 12-min run | VO₂ max: men ≥35 min, aim 50+; women ≥30; independence line = 18 |
| Long duration | 20+ min steady non-walk, ideally nasal only |

Battery yearly minimum (6 months ideal), spread over ~3 days: body comp first (48h no hard exercise), fatiguing tests last, anaerobic + VO₂max on own days.

## Principles
- **Train your worst score most often** ("performance anchors" — raise the anchor and the whole ship sails faster).
- Strength-before-endurance rule: if you can't do one rep, endurance training won't create it.
- Power deficits: train from YOUR force-velocity curve, not generic "30% 1RM."
- Reliability beats validity for self-tracking.
- Most bad gym assumptions came from bodybuilding culture post-1977, not data.

# EP2 — Strength & Muscle Growth

## Definitions
Strength = function (produce force); hypertrophy = size only. They diverge via neural mechanisms: firing rate, motor-unit sync, ACh recycling, calcium handling, fiber-type shift, pennation angle, PCr storage.

Aging numbers: size −1%/yr after 40 · strength −2–4%/yr · **power −8–10%/yr** (the real functional threat) · total motor units −30–40%. Age-90+ subjects gained 30–170% size in ~12 weeks — decline comes from lost training, not aging. Strength training = the ONLY tool against neuromuscular aging. Tendons adapt slowly ("tissue tolerance" is what returning athletes lack); bone best built by axial loading when young.

## Three hypertrophy mechanisms
Mechanical tension · metabolic stress · muscle damage. **Damage NOT required** — "a flat-out lie that you must break a muscle down to grow." You need ONE mechanism delivered loud enough once, frequent enough, or combined. The only failing combo: infrequent + light + low volume. This breadth is why every style from HIT to high-volume works.

Protein alone is anabolic (30 g fasted raises MPS ~4–5 h); training stimulus and feeding are independent pathways that stack. Sarcoplasmic hypertrophy rehabilitated by research; muscle memory likely **epigenetic** ("nuclei remember how to ride a bike").

## Strength protocol — "the 3-to-5 rule"
3–5 days/wk · 3–5 exercises · 3–5 reps/set · 3–5 sets · **3–5 min rest** · load **≥70% 1RM** (below ~60% doesn't count as a working set) · compound movement patterns incl. ROTATION (neglected!) · always first/fresh · tempo 3-1-1 explode · progression +3–5%/wk for ~5 weeks then deload · advanced tools: cluster sets, bands/chains, eccentric overload >100% 1RM · intent overrides load (max-effort planks build strength at ~50% 1RM) · keep exercises identical ≥6–12 weeks.

## Hypertrophy protocol
Volume is king: floor 10 sets/muscle/wk (maintenance), target **15–20**, advanced toward 25+. Count primary+secondary movers (chin-up biceps count, pull-up biceps don't). Reps 4–30 work; optimal **8–15**; stop at **~RIR 2**. Failure optional/strategic — safe machines or last exercise; novices should experience it once to calibrate. Rest ≤~2 min practical. Frequency barely matters at equated volume — prefer **3×/week full-body** for life-resilience; 30–40-min sessions suffice. Skip plyometrics and Olympic lifts for growth. Plateau → usually MORE volume, or flip to the opposite rep spectrum.

## Verdicts with numbers
- Speed: strength = accelerate maximally; hypertrophy = controlled any cadence.
- Mind-muscle connection real/trainable — lats take months-years; pre-exhaustion bridges.
- Soreness scale: >3/10 investigate, >6/10 don't train it.
- Cold immersion blocks the hypertrophy signaling cascade — not on growth days.
- Interference matters only via energy balance or shared eccentric damage (run > cycle).
- Static stretching pre-lift cuts power ~10%; supersets cost ~5% strength performance.
- Blood pressure in heavy sets can hit ~450/350 mmHg — release pressure carefully.

## Takeaways
Define target adaptation first · 3-to-5 for strength · 15–20 sets @ 8–15 reps @ RIR2 for growth · +3–5%/wk progression · lock exercises 6–12 wks · rotate neglected patterns (rotation!) · 48–72h between same-muscle sessions · track HRV/RHR/motivation trends of 3–5 days · protein ~1 g/lb makes timing debates moot · creatine 3–6 g/day forever · no phone mid-session.

# EP3 — Endurance & Fat Loss

## Six endurance capacities
All-day energy · muscular endurance (local, 5–50 reps) · max anaerobic (20–80 s) · max aerobic (5–15 min) · sustained position (20–40 min posture) · long duration (finish feeling good).

Endurance = **fatigue management + fueling** — misdiagnosing which one failed you is the common error.

## Energy systems (the carbon game)
Oxygen exists to exhale CO₂. The campfire analogy:
- **Match** = phosphocreatine (0–20 s, 1:1 ATP)
- **Newspaper** = glycolysis (~10 s–2 min); lactate is a BUFFER and premium FUEL (Cori cycle), not the burn's cause
- **Log** = fat (unlimited stores, too slow — never the limiter; systemic, so no spot reduction)
- **Metal rail** = protein (burns badly, destroys structure; ~10% of energy at best)

Systems are complementary bicycle gears — higher intensity = higher carb %. Even elite marathoners burn up to ~80% carbs.

## Protocols
- **Breathing gears**: nasal-only fixed cadence → nasal any cadence → mouth allowed. Most can't exceed ~70–80% effort nasally.
- **Muscular endurance**: train the exact task at/slightly above target load, ~3×/wk, +1–2 reps weekly.
- **Anaerobic capacity**: air bike/rower/uphill/sled; bouts 20–90 s; rest to nasal-only breathing; ≥4 rounds × 3 days/wk; cap true-max days at 3–4/wk.
- **Max aerobic**: 1-mile all-out test weekly + ~40% support work between Zone 2 and mile pace.
- **Long duration**: ≥20–30 min steady state weekly; nasal cooldown doubles as down-regulation.
- Whole-week totals ≈ **2 hours covering all four types** — Huberman's dose: ~5–6 min total all-out per week (matches Gibala: equal-or-better VO₂max vs 180 min).

## Fat-loss truth
"Calories in/out" is really **carbon in/carbon out**. Substrate burned during exercise is irrelevant to net loss (everything funnels through the same ETC). Fasted cardio doesn't enhance loss; fat-burning-zone logic collapses (sleeping is the highest %-fat activity). Bonk = liver glycogen death (>~2 h). New muscle burns only ~6–10 kcal/day/lb. Train fuel flexibility: carbs before high-intensity sessions; fasted easy sessions as adaptation investment. Avoid big fast-carb boluses immediately pre-exercise (insulin double-whammy).

## Cheap metrics
RHR <60 · RER >1.1 confirms true VO₂max · fasting glucose ≤85 · standard-loop test weekly (stable fasted time/HR/RPE = good fat flexibility) · technical breakdown ends any endurance set, not exhaustion.

# EP4 — Programming for Fitness & Longevity

Core claim: **any written plan beats no plan** — adherence and progressive overload both require one.

## Galpin's 10-step design process
1. Assess → ONE SMART goal **minus 10%** (deception study: goals ±5% motivate; ±15% make people quit)
2. Identify your "Defenders" (why you failed before) + score the **life quadrant**: 10 points across Business/Relationships/Fitness/Recovery — recovery ≥ half of fitness, ≥2/10 total; attach concrete rules ("no work after 7 PM Thu–Sun") and an accountability partner
3. Calendar life dates FIRST, plan training backward
4. Underestimate available days (count door-to-door: a "45-min workout" is ~90–150 min of life)
5. Exercises balanced across the WEEK; every pick gets a progression strategy; **complexity ladder before load**: assistance → bodyweight → eccentric → iso-hold → concentric → speed → fatigue
6. Priority goes FIRST on your most consistent day (chaos management; one MLB client ran a 9-day training cycle all season)
7. Progression: intensity ~+3%/wk, volume ≤+10%/wk (prefers 5–7%); compute weekly total reps
8. Rest: 2–5 min speed/power/strength; endurance rests mirror the target quality
9. Pre-mortem chaos management: predict failure points now ("before we hit submit, we sleep on it")

## Annual quarter template
~6 weeks loading → deload to 70% → repeat; formal deloads every 4–8 weeks; 12-week block = 5 hard + deload week + 5 hard + week 12 fully off.

- **Q1 (Jan–Mar): Hypertrophy** — +10–15% calories above baseline (not a dirty bulk), lift 3–4×/wk, indoor sport
- **Q2 (Apr–Jun): Fat loss** — hypocaloric, outdoor sport, fitness class, lift 2×/wk maintenance
- **Q3 (Jul–Sep): Conditioning** — maintenance calories, two outdoor sports 2×/wk, sprint work 2×/wk, lift 2×/wk
- **Q4 (Oct–Dec): Endurance** — calories up again, lifting drops to 1×/wk, machine intervals

## Concurrent-training compatibility
Speed+power+strength fully compatible · strength+hypertrophy compete only near maximal levels · interference is ONE-directional (hypertrophy fatigue hurts speed/power) · strength/power IMPROVES endurance · endurance neutral-to-hurts strength · threshold ≈ >30 min at >60% maxHR · cycling/swimming beat running for interference · buy back interference with food/recovery.

## Longevity prescriptions
Health combine to preserve forever: grip strength, leg strength, muscle mass, speed/power (fall recovery), VO₂max. ≥1 proprioception session/wk (linked to dementia/Parkinson's prevention). Keep sprinting permanently. ~5 sets/wk maintains strength 8+ weeks during low phases. Zone-5 work ≥30 min/wk helps deep sleep if ≥6 h from bedtime. Physical activity (150–200 min walking) ≠ structured exercise — need both.

## Sample minimal week (3 days × 45 min)
D1: speed/power first, then hypertrophy · D2: pure strength + high-HR finisher · D3: long steady state.

# EP5 — Recovery

Stress → adaptation ONLY IF recovery outpaces stress. Training is the trigger; results happen in recovery.

## Four levels
1. **Acute overload** — one hard bout; minutes-to-days recovery; this is what you want.
2. **Functional overreaching** — accumulated overload; after a few days–1 week, performance ENHANCED (why tapers work).
3. **Non-functional overreaching** — weeks just to reach baseline again; the "no results → train harder → blow up" trap.
4. **Overtraining** — months to recover; rare; no clinical test exists (retroactively diagnosed).

**Adaptation vs optimization trade-off**: NSAIDs/ice/high-dose antioxidants/cortisol-blunters make today better and block the adaptation signal. Choose per phase — in-season = acute recovery; base phase = protect adaptation.

## Monitoring system
- Daily: 1 subjective (mood/motivation) + 1 objective (HRV or CO₂-tolerance test), same conditions every time; build 4–6 weeks baseline before acting; compare same-weekday-to-same-weekday.
- Act only on the **triad**: performance ↓ + physiology ↓ + symptoms ↓, persisting **≥5 consecutive days** (HRV: ~7 days) → cut training ~50% until rebound toward baseline.
- Quarterly bloods: testosterone, cortisol CURVE (~3 draws/day), T:C ratio, SHBG (rises with overtraining and calorie restriction). Semiannual: glutamine ratio, IL-6/TNF-α, neutrophil:lymphocyte (>9:1 alarm).
- Speed/power tests (vertical jump, $20–40 grip dynamometer) degrade FIRST — better canaries than strength maxes. Power −8–10%/yr with age mirrors fatigue.

## Modality verdict table

| Tool | Verdict | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Down-regulation breathing | Top pick, zero cost | Box breathing or cyclic sighing 3–10 min post-workout, lying down, eyes covered; extended EXHALE is the active ingredient; RCT: 5 min/day lowered resting HR + raised HRV |
| Music | Works | Switch to slow-paced immediately post-workout |
| Compression garments | Safe, mild positive | Wear ASAP post-session and on flights; does NOT block adaptation |
| Pneumatic boots/percussion/massage | Fine, interchangeable | None block long-term adaptation |
| Cold immersion | Best soreness relief; COSTS hypertrophy | <40°F ~5 min or 40–50°F >15 min; ~65°F useless; cold showers don't count; ice = sympathetic stressor, HRV crashes then rebounds above baseline hours later |
| Heat/sauna/hot bath | Galpin's favorite | Feels worse in the moment, great next day; benchmark 57 min/wk; males conceiving avoid ~60 days |
| Contrast hot-cold | Anecdotal only | No validated dosing |
| NSAIDs/anti-inflammatories | Pain rescue ONLY | Cortisol spike + inflammatory pulse are REQUIRED for adaptation; never prophylactic |

## Key mechanisms & facts
Nocturnal epinephrine up ~50% explains overtraining insomnia (kills REM). Carbs at night lower cortisol → easier sleep. Bright early light = ~50% larger morning cortisol pulse (desirable). Fry lab: daily 1RM squats × 2 weeks → power −35%, β-adrenergic receptors downregulated ~37%, nocturnal epinephrine +50%; 2–8 weeks back to baseline. Speed dies before strength (in aging AND fatigue) → use velocity/power tests for early warning. Ashwagandha/rhodiola blunt/modulate cortisol — strategic use only if tested high, not foundational.

"Widen the lane": deliberately absorb imperfect conditions periodically so resilience machinery keeps adapting instead of shrinking.

# EP6 — Nutrition & Supplements

Hierarchy: behavior/sleep/hydration → food → supplements LAST. Goal state = needing almost none.

## Foundations
- **Protein**: ≥1 g/lb (2.2 g/kg) during injury/heavy phases; whey gold standard, vegan fine. Daily TOTAL beats timing.
- Peri-workout rule: ~0.5 g carbs/lb + ~0.25 g protein/lb spread pre/mid/post; scale carb:protein to session cost — easy day 1:1 (~50–75 g carbs), brutal/hot/long 3–4:1 (~150–200 g).
- Injury: BMR rises up to +10% → raise calories ~10%, keep macros stable.
- Carb loading = 3–4 gradual days, not one pasta bowl.
- Fasted training permissible if glycogen topped off — but "possible" ≠ "optimal"; no scenario where fasting improves performance.
- Intra-workout carbs (60–100 g/hr, glucose:fructose ≥2:1, 5–9% solution) ONLY for >2 h or supramaximal sessions.
- Stalled progress? Taper first (−50% volume for ~1 week per 8 trained; hold intensity/frequency) → expect 3–8% performance gain within days.

## Supplement stack table

| Supplement | Dose | Timing | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creatine monohydrate | 3–5 g/day (up to ~10 g big athletes) | Any time, chronic (3–4 wks to saturate) | STRONG #1: strength, recovery, bone density, cognition/mood, sleep-deprivation offset |
| Fish oil/omega-3 | 2–5 g/day EPA+DHA ~1:1 | Daily | Moderate-strong inflammation management; >15 g harms immunity |
| Vitamin D | per bloodwork | Daily | Well-researched |
| Multivitamin | 1 serving | Daily | Covers A + zinc (remodeling micronutrients) |
| Magnesium | ~6 mg/kg BW/day | Evening common | Citrate most evidence |
| Electrolytes | ~3:1 Na:K; match sweat (500 mg–3.5 g Na/session varies) | Sweaty/long sessions | Strong in heat |
| Caffeine | 1–3 mg/kg (~30 min pre) | Pre-session | Strong endurance; mixed strength; >5 mg/kg decrements |
| Beetroot/nitrate | Label | Pre-endurance | Moderate; non-stimulant (sleep-safe) |
| Alpha-GPC | 300–600 mg | Key sessions only | Limited data; focus aid |
| Rhodiola | Label | Acute | Cortisol modulator — caution if already flat |
| Beta-alanine | Titrate 2→5–6 g/day over weeks | Daily chronic | Strong acid-buffering; tingling fades |
| Glutamine | 20 g/day split AM/PM | Recovery phases | Moderate gut/recovery support |
| Curcumin | 500 mg ×3/day | With recovery meals | Anti-inflammatory; LOWERS DHT (libido crash risk) |
| Tart cherry | Label | Evening | DOMS + modest sleep aid |

**Skip**: alkaline water · BCAAs/EAAs (if protein adequate) · random probiotics · multi-ingredient blends (underdosed actives; melatonin labels off by up to 1000×) · prophylactic antioxidants/anti-inflammatories · creatine loading cycles.

## Hydration equations
1. Baseline: **0.5 oz fluid/lb BW/day** (all fluids count).
2. Intra-workout: **BW(lb) ÷ 30 = oz every 15–20 min** (sip, never chug).
3. Post: drink back **125% of weighed losses**.
Self-check = WUT: Weight (morning), Urine color, Thirst. −2% BW dehydration already impairs skill; overhydration (hyponatremia) mimics it — seen in 2–15% of race finishers. Night waking >2×/night = diagnostic: large clear voids → cut water 2h pre-bed; small voids → vasopressin/sleep flag. Whole foods carry water; cutting processed food means re-adding salt deliberately.

## Practical takeaways
Behavior first, always · creatine forever · protein 1 g/lb · hydration trio daily · single-ingredient third-party-tested products only · change one variable at a time (n-of-1 experiments) · protect the inflammatory window post-session (no ice/NSAIDs/antioxidants then) · taper before buying more supplements · fix sleep environment before buying anything.

# RP Hypertrophy Series — Study Notes

Distilled from Renaissance Periodization's 11-video series "How To Design A Muscle Growth Training Program" (Dr. Mike Israetel, ft. RP Hypertrophy App). Raw transcripts archived in [transcripts/](transcripts/) — read the notes first, watch any video for depth. Playlist: the one Najib saved (RP channel).

## How to use

Watch one video per sitting → read its notes here after → apply that week in GymLog. The series teaches ONE thing: how to run a **mesocycle** — a ~4-week training block (3 weeks loading + 1 week deload), then repeat with slightly heavier starts.

## Video 1 — Designing your training split

- Pick days you can **actually** hit every week; overestimating adherence = missed days = disappointment. Honest 3 beats aspirational 5.
- Days per week by training age: beginner (<2 yrs) 2–3 · intermediate (2–5 yrs) 3–5 · advanced (5+ yrs) 4–6.
- Low frequencies have the best results-per-effort ratio; add days only when recovery is easy AND you crave more.
- Max **4–6 muscle groups per day** — quality dies past that.
- **Priority rule**: whatever matters most to you goes FIRST in the day (most energy = best stimulus).
- Space the same muscle symmetrically (e.g., Mon+Thu, not Mon+Tue) so it recovers between hits.
- Watch hidden overlap: pressing days tire triceps — don't program triceps the day before chest.

## Video 2 — Exercise selection

- Rank exercises by **stimulus-to-fatigue ratio (SFR)**: target muscle gets tension/burn/pump/soreness, joints stay happy, psychological cost low. Enjoyment counts — research links liking an exercise to adherence and results.
- Pick exercises you can **progress on for months**; if gains stall on it quickly, swap it.
- "Muscle confusion" is a myth — the body doesn't guess. Old favorites that check the boxes are fine forever.
- No wrong answers exist among proper exercises; don't overthink variety.

## Video 3 — Warm-ups & load selection

Skip general cardio warm-up if you want (overrated); then a ~6-step ramp to the working weight:

1. Optional general warm-up (few minutes, optional).
2. Lightest load (~30-rep-max weight): **12 reps**, perfect technique, full ROM.
3. Add weight (~20RM load): **8 reps**. Now gauge the weight honestly.
4. Best guess at working weight: do only **4 reps**, rest, judge ("too heavy? too light?").
5. First working set ≈ near that probe weight, stop at **2–3 RIR**, log reps.
- Rest properly between every warm-up step — rushing warm-ups causes sloppy technique and injury risk.
- Anywhere from 5 to 30 reps per set builds muscle when close to failure.
- Exact reps in week 1 don't matter — just land inside your chosen rep range.

## Video 4 — Estimating reps in reserve (RIR)

- 2 RIR = the moment the bar suddenly feels much heavier/slower ("the weight pushes back").
- Slow-grinders: your grind phase can last several reps at similar speed — keep going until it gets *noticeably* slower/heavier, THEN rack.
- Undershooting slightly is nearly free: stimulus stays high, technique stays crisp.
- Overshooting early poisons the whole mesocycle: fatigue accumulates like snowfall (no melting mid-block) and steals weeks 2–3 PRs.
- Week 1 effort sets your ceiling — leave reps in the tank, let progression climb weekly (+1 rep or +~5 lb).
- Don't obsess over exact RIR; "challenged but not buried" is the operating zone.

## Video 5 — Too heavy or too light?

- 5–30 rep sets all work; there are better/worse answers but few wrong ones.
- The **target muscle must be the limiting factor**: squats >15 reps usually fail via lower-back/lungs, not quads → go heavier/lower-rep for such lifts.
- Isolation work (curls, laterals) feels best at 10–20+; compounds tolerate/heavy-low fine (load spreads across joints).
- Slightly outside 5–30 occasionally is harmless; sets of 50+ are where stimulus genuinely drops.
- Change load by small steps; big jumps break progression tracking.

## Video 6 — Training modalities

- **Straight sets**: normal set, full rest (~1–3 min), same weight across sets.
- **Down sets**: drop weight ~10–20% after fatigue to stay in the target rep range instead of grinding out 5-rep sets.
- **Drop sets**: ~0 rest, cut weight 10–20%, chase 5–10 more reps, repeat 3–6 tiers. Finishers — fatiguing, use sparingly.
- **Giant sets** (RP definition): pick a total-rep goal (e.g., 50), reach it with mini-sets + normal rests; great for practicing mind-muscle connection without pressure to match weekly numbers.
- **Myo-reps**: one set near failure → rest 3–5 breaths → mini-set of 5–10 near failure → repeat. Removes "junk lead-in reps"; every mini-set is money reps.
- **Myo-match sets**: first straight set sets the number (e.g., 16); every later set must re-hit 16 using myo-breaks. Massive effective volume — only for durable muscles (side/rear delts, forearms, calves); never for deadlifts/squats.
- **Supersets**: antagonist pairs (curls/triceps) save time with minimal hypertrophy cost.

## Video 7 — Technique & joint pain

- Good technique = target muscle stimulated AND joints stable, comfortable, not weakening — verified over **multiple sessions**, not one.
- If pain appears: first fix technique (watch demos), then micro-adjust (grip width one finger, foot angle, pull-to-clavicle vs upper-chest) — tiny changes often erase pain completely.
- Israetel's skull-crusher fix: break at the elbow toward the hip FIRST, then lower — elbow pain gone forever. Small hinges, big doors.
- Chronic escalating joint pain is never "part of bodybuilding" — replace the exercise rather than wrap it and push.

## Video 8 — Pump & soreness as signals

- Soreness is NOT required for growth. Read it as a volume dial:
  - Never sore + not growing → add sets.
  - A little sore, healed before next hit → volume is right.
  - Still sore at next session, every week → cut sets (chronic overlap soreness).
- Pump is a proxy for stimulus: no pump at all likely means understimulated; good pump = on track; psychotic pump = done, adding more only adds damage.
- Rate yourself HONESTLY against these signals (in GymLog: use workout notes/history) — lying to inflate or deflate numbers corrupts your own auto-regulation.

## Video 9 — Progression

- Pre-plan small jumps: **+1 rep or +~5 lb (≈5%) per week** per exercise. That's the whole engine of growth.
- Missed target? Normal — you get slack before a deload is due. Two consecutive down-weeks = deload signal.
- Blowing past targets mid-block steals fatigue budget from week 3–4; save max efforts for the final week before deload.
- Small steady PRs beat hero sets. "Don't show off, get a little bit more."

## Video 10 — Deloads (why stopping builds)

- Fatigue accumulates as: micro-tears, hormonal drift (testosterone ↓ cortisol ↑), neural fatigue (~90–95% recovered per week → compounds), glycogen depletion, coordination loss.
- High chronic fatigue flips metabolism catabolic (AMPK up, mTOR sensitivity down) — you train as hard as ever and grow ~nothing while feeling roughly the same size. Silent progress killer.
- Most hard-training lifters need a deload every **3–6 weeks**: same week structure, markedly less weight/sets/effort (RIR huge).
- Pro tip: condense deload days (Mon+Tue into one easy session, Thu+Fri into another) — gym twice, recover fully, come back ferocious.

## Video 11 — Your next mesocycle

- After deload, rebuild: reuse exercises you enjoyed, progressed on, and that spared your joints; vary the rest.
- Restart loads ≈ **week-1 load of last meso +~5%** (advanced: +2–2.5%). Conservative start = room to climb again.
- ~6 mesos/year at +40 lb cumulative on a lift is enormous — never chase load at the cost of technique/mind-muscle connection; sometimes improved technique *lowers* your numbers while growth accelerates.

## Cheat sheet (print this)

1. Train 3–5 days honest · 4–6 muscle groups/day · priority muscle first · symmetric spacing.
2. Exercises = high SFR + progressive + joint-friendly + enjoyable. Keep winners.
3. Warm-up ramp: 12 light → 8 medium → 4 probe → work. Rest between steps.
4. Working sets: 5–30 reps, stop at 2–3 RIR (week 1), muscle must be limiter.
5. Progress +1 rep or ~+5%/week. Two flat/down weeks → deload.
6. Deload every 3–6 weeks: half effort, condense days, eat, sleep.
7. Signals: never-sore-and-stuck = add sets · overlapping soreness = cut sets · no pump = underworked · insane pump = done.
8. New meso: favorite exercises, restart ~5% above last block's week 1.

GymLog covers: logging weights×reps per set, PR detection (estimated 1RM), history, weekly volume chart. It does NOT yet auto-program RIR targets, mesocycle progression, or deload scheduling — apply this series' rules manually using the cheat sheet during the trial.

# RP Muscle Specialization Series — Study Notes

Distilled from Renaissance Periodization's 13-video "Muscle Growth Specialization Programs" series (Dr. Mike Israetel). Raw transcripts archived in [transcripts/](transcripts/). Companion to [../rp-series/NOTES.md](../rp-series/NOTES.md) — read that first for mesocycle basics (RIR, deloads, progression).

## The universal specialization template (video 1)

Specialization = pushing ONE (or two) lagging muscles hard for ~3 mesocycles (~3 months), funded by cutting competing volume. Never specialize everything at once — work up from one muscle and find your recovery limit (~4–5 max).

The recipe, identical across all 13 videos:

1. **Specificity** — pick exercises that bias the target region (e.g., vertical pulls + lat prayers for upper-outer lats, not rows).
2. **Priority placement** — target muscle trained FIRST while fresh, on most days that touch it.
3. **High frequency (short-term)** — 3–4x/week for the target beats 1–2x when catching up; doesn't need to be sustainable.
4. **Cut synergists** — reduce muscles that share the movement (biceps/triceps/low-back for pulling days) so the target is always the limiter, not a tired helper.
5. **Pulsatile week**: heavy smash early-week → deliberately light late-week session → recover → repeat. Friday-easy keeps Monday-fresh.
6. **Volume ramp**: start 2–6 sets/session near MEV; add sets ONLY if fully recovered before the next hit; final pre-deload week ≈ 0 RIR with peak sets.
7. **Progression**: +2.5–5 lb or +1–2 reps weekly; RIR drifts 3→0 across the block; two straight failed sessions = deload now.
8. **Exit**: after ~3 mesos take active rest / low-volume maintenance (~1–3 sets, 2x/week) before the next block.

## Video 1 — Upper/outer lats (back width)

- Vertical pulling (weighted pull-ups) + straight-arm pulldowns/lat prayers target upper-outer lats better than rows; keep rows later/lighter for general back.
- Back 3–4x/week: Mon/Wed focused smash, Fri only ~2 light rowing sets so weekend recovery is full.
- Start 2–6 total back sets/session depending on training age; heavy 5–10 first when fresh, isolation later.
- Sample week-4/5 jump: sets rise ~3–5 total, RIR hits 0 — then deload.
- After 3 mesos: 2 weeks active rest or ~4 weeks maintenance before the next block.

## Video 2 — Upper chest

- Core: incline barbell press 5–10, incline cable flies 10–20, low-incline DB press 10–20; flat/machine presses kept but later, lighter. Skip gimmick grips unless loadable+progressable.
- Chest 3–4x/week, upper-pec-first in 2–3 of them; cut triceps/front-delts so they never cap presses. Fri = easy flat-only maintenance (~3–4 sets).
- Start 2–6 sets/session @3 RIR; +1 set only when fully recovered; sample last week ≈7 incline sets @0 RIR. Two straight failed sessions → deload.
- Insight: the intentionally easy Friday makes growth pulsatile — trash Mon/Wed, drain fatigue Fri — sustainable across weeks instead of burning out by week 2.

## Video 3 — Side delts

- Lateral raises in all forms (standard, super-ROM, cable Y-raises); upright rows + face pulls secondary; 10–15 up to 20–30 reps — skip 5–10 if shoulders complain.
- 3–4x/week (Mon/Wed/Fri/Sat), ALWAYS first, even before chest; cut front-delt pressing and trap volume; Sat slightly lighter.
- Low damage muscle: can ramp toward 6–10 sets/session by meso end; myo-reps (15–25) appear late-block because specialization demands the best tools even when uncomfortable.
- Insight: side delts are nearly impossible to damage — they tolerate 4x/week hard training that would wreck most muscles.

## Video 4 — Triceps

- Triceps ≈ two-thirds of upper-arm mass. Heavy extension core: skull crushers 5–10 (+down sets 10–15), overhead extensions 5–10 & 10–15, pushdowns 20–25+, incline close-grip bench 10–15, weighted dips 10–15.
- Frequency ladder: 2x/week first meso → 3 → 4. Place triceps right BEFORE chest days or after easy chest days — never hard triceps then hard chest next day. Cut chest/front-delt volume hard.
- Start 2–6 sets/session; never open with 12 sets 3x/week — damage steals recovery and blunts overload. Drop-set extras add little fatigue, freer to add.
- Shift rep ranges up each meso (5–10 → 10–20 → 20–30) as frequency rises, sparing elbows.
- Insight: holding new size costs far less than building it ("maintaining conquered land") — unsustainable blitzes are fine; 1–2x/week keeps gains after.

## Video 5 — Quads

- High-stimulus compounds: leg press, hack squat 10–15, high-bar squat 5–10, extensions 20–25, short-step deep walking lunges 20–25 steps/leg.
- Only 2–3x/week possible — quads generate the body's LARGEST systemic fatigue; cut one more muscle group than seems necessary elsewhere.
- Start 2–6 sets/session, stop at first pump sign early-block (48h session recurrence + soreness spike risk); build to ~7–8 sets by pre-deload.
- Hack squat BEFORE back squat = pre-exhaustion: lighter squats become quad-limited, systemic cost drops. Prefer small load jumps over rep additions (reps silently worsen stimulus-to-fatigue).
- Insight: quad specialization buys the biggest slice of the recovery pie — paid for by deeper cuts everywhere else.

## Video 6 — Forearms

- Flexion core: barbell wrist curls with top pause 10–20, one-arm bench wrist curls 10–20, gripper closes 10–20 (count only full metal-on-metal closes). Extensors skipped — back/shoulder work covers them.
- 3–5x/week always FIRST; wear straps/Versa grips for ALL other pulling to erase grip interference. Pair forearm days with pull days.
- One exercise per workout (variation scarce); start 2–6 sets, ramp to 7–8; short ROM means short tension time → 10–20+ reps beat low reps; rest just until burn clears (~3 min for 4 quality sets).
- Insight: high-rep back work where lats fail long before grip is junk volume for forearms — straps surgically delete that dead zone so forearms arrive fresh at their own training.

## Video 7 — Lower chest

- Flat BB press arched/retracted 5–10, paused weighted dips feet-BEHIND + forward lean + deep pause, decline cable flies, flat machine press; low-incline DB on easy day.
- Normal chest frequency +1; 2 of 3 sessions lower-pec-focused; cut triceps (e.g., 15 → 10 sets/wk) so they never fail before pecs on dips.
- Dip form decides the target: feet behind + lean = chest dip; feet front + upright = triceps dip.
- Insight: arch + retracted scapulae on plain flat presses exposes lower chest so well that decline benches are mostly redundant.

## Video 8 — Calves

- Multi-angle standing raises (machine/hack/leg-press/stair+dumbbell); skip seated raises (bent knee = gastroc inactive, soleus-only "cankle" look). First set 15–25 reps.
- Near-zero systemic cost → 3–4x/week easy; train early, never right before quads; full session ≈5–10 minutes.
- Start 2–3 sets/session, build to 5–8 via myo-reps late-block; slow eccentric, full stretch, no bouncing.
- Insight: sets of 5–10 fail because tiny ROM gives almost no tension time — moderately-heavy 15–30-rep sets beat Arnold-style maximal weights.

## Video 9 — Glutes

- Deep-stretch compounds are the money: lunges/Bulgarian split squats, wide-stance sumo squats, deficit deadlifts; hip thrusts for peak contraction; abductor machine peppered in.
- Big expensive muscle → put quads/hams near maintenance; glutes fresh-first on leg days + own day; heavier early week.
- Start literally ONE lunge set (instant soreness risk), autoregulate up; hold thrust lockout 1–2 s, slow eccentrics.
- Insight: ego hip-thrusts (405×5) shift work to hams/adductors/low-back — lighter weight + mind-muscle + 15–20 reps actually grows glutes.

## Video 10 — Arms (specialization)

- Rotate 3–5 biceps + 3–5 triceps movements; test each across 5–10 / 10–20 / 20–30 for your best stimulus-to-fatigue. Stretch-biased picks: incline curls, behind-neck extensions.
- Train whenever recovered (every 1–3 days); arms FIRST even before big lifts. With everything else at ~1/3 volume, arm tolerance climbs to 25–35+ sets/week/muscle.
- Post-exhaust pairings turn big lifts into arm work: underhand pulldowns after curls; close-grip push-ups after extensions.
- Insight: no single "best arm exercise" exists — rotate effective ones as staleness appears, and eat (+0.25–0.5 lb/week) or no program can be judged fairly.

## Video 11 — Rear delts

- Bent/rear lateral raises, face pulls, cross-body cable laterals (tension under stretch); 10–20, often 20–30 reps — heavy 5s strain the joint with zero pump.
- Small fast-recovering muscle: 3–4x/week (up to 6), ideal FIRST specialization; train early (back → rear delts → back).
- Rarely gets sore → judge recovery by next-session PERFORMANCE, not soreness. Strict form mandatory — side delts/lats/teres steal the motion easily.
- Insight: full-ROM back training alone may already supply most rear-delt stimulus; add direct sets only when lagging despite good back work.

## Video 12 — Hamstrings

- Leg curls at multiple angles (lying/seated/standing 10–20), hip hinges (SLDL 5–10 — high reps exhaust low-back first), Nordics/GHR 5–10.
- Max 2–3x/week (4+ forces baby workouts); hams FIRST even before squats; lighten glutes/quads/low-back that sabotage hinges; heaviest Monday.
- 2–6 sets/session, rarely >6–7 even pre-deload; elite technique/mind-muscle shrinks required volume ~⅓. GHR: start ≤2 sets; stop SLDL 1–2 shy of failure.
- Insight: hamstrings take extreme mechanical damage (first-time 3×12 SLDL = 2.5 weeks sore) — low-volume ultra-quality beats 20-set pro routines.

## Video 13 — Adductors

- Paused sumo squats, wide-stance leg press (toes out, deep), adductor machine (slow eccentric, stretch pause, top squeeze), deficit pause sumo DLs, walking lunges (first session: ONE set of 15–20).
- Convert existing quad days into quad+adductor days — never add separate days (double exposures = disaster); ease off glutes/hams which already recruit adductors.
- Never train through overlapping soreness (knees-together test); machine work = isolation tool with pauses; finish quads via myo-rep leg extensions once adductors fried.
- Insight: adductors are perfectly leveraged to stimulate AND tear — cap at 2–3 mesos then return to normal legs.

# How this feeds GymLog (analysis only — NO app changes yet)

These videos describe mechanics GymLog v1 does not model. Mapped to potential v2 features, in rough priority order:

| RP concept | GymLog gap today | Possible feature |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly sets per MUSCLE GROUP | App counts sets per exercise; no muscle rollup | Volume dashboard: sets/week per muscle vs MEV–MRV bands |
| Specialization blocks | No concept of a "focus muscle" or block | Pick focus muscle → app flags its priority order + suggests cutting synergist volume |
| RIR targets drifting 3→0 per week | Sets record weight×reps only | Optional RIR field per set + weekly target shown |
| Deload trigger (2 failed weeks / every 3–6 wks) | Nothing tracks it | Auto-flag after two down-weeks; scheduled deload reminder |
| Pump/soreness/joint-pain check-ins | None | 3-tap post-workout questionnaire storing history (feeds #7) |
| Exercise SFR memory ("which exercises work best for me") | PRs tracked, but no stimulus/pump linkage | Per-exercise effectiveness score from logged feedback + progress rate |
| Synergist conflict warnings | Program is static PPL | Warn e.g. heavy triceps day-before-chest |
| Maintenance volume for non-focus muscles | N/A | Presets when a specialization block is active |
