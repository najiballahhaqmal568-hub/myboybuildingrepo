## The four-level planning pyramid [01]

| Level | Timeframe | Decides | Analogy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual plan | 52 weeks | Direction + key dates | Compass |
| Phases | months | Sequence toward peaks | Map |
| Mesocycle | 3–6 weeks | Overload + deload | Route for next stretch |
| Microcycle | 1 week | Exercises, sets, reps, loads | Actually walking |

Key discipline: **each level only holds what it can know**. The annual plan never contains exact weights/reps — plans change (injury, travel), so exact prescriptions are written only in the near term. Plan broad far away, detailed close up.

## Phase potentiation [03]

Sequence phases so each builds on the last:

- **Accumulation**: volume ↑, intensity flat → work capacity + tolerance for later phases.
- **Intensification**: volume held, load ↑ → converts tissue into performance.
- **Realization**: volume ↓↓, intensity ↑↑ → sharpest expression; usable only sparingly — too many realization phases and there's no accumulated base to express.
- For a bodybuilder: life happens in accumulation; the other two exist when a date demands expressed strength (or not at all).

## Planning the week (microcycle) [05]

- **Pair similar qualities on the same day** (max-speed with fast-power; upper strength with upper endurance) to avoid interference effects between days.
- Rest day goes AFTER the week's most intense session; active recovery there beats low-quality training.
- Before an important event: least fatiguing work only.
- When external demand rises (in-season equivalent), cut volume first, keep quality — train "as much as possible without overreaching," which shrinks as life stress grows.

## New takeaways beyond the periodization series

1. The **compass→map→route→walking** mental model for who-plans-what-at-which-horizon.
2. **Anti-overplanning rule**: never write exact numbers more than ~a block ahead.
3. Quality-pairing within training days to dodge interference (for GymLog's future: cardio placement advice).
4. Explicit framing that deload frequency should scale with stress: high-CNS work → shorter blocks.
